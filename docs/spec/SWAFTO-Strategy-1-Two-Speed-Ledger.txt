Strategy 1 -- The Two-Speed Ledger

One day record, two paths to it, different navigation costs and identical data. This
document specifies exactly how every screen and component behaves on each path,
what the single origin parameter controls, and the eight edge cases that decide
whether the reconciliation can be trusted.

01 The principle

A sale entered at 11 a.m. and the same sale entered during the evening close are one record.
The paths to that record must not cost the same, because they are used in completely
different circumstances -- one hand on the phone with a customer waiting, versus sitting down
at the end of the day.

    The rule: the six rails on Today open the entry form directly, and Done returns to Today. The
    day-close list opens the same form one tap later, and Done returns to the list. Two speeds, one
    form, one record. Anything that captures a transaction and writes it somewhere other than the day
    record is a defect, however convenient it looks.

Both paths reach one form and write one record. The rails are a shorter route to the same place, never a separate
feature.

02 Path A -- rail capture, during the day

Used twenty to forty times a day, one-handed, with a customer waiting. Every tap here is
multiplied by the number of transactions, which is why it is specified to the tap.

03 Path B -- close review, at the end of the day

Used once. Its job is not fast entry; its job is to let the shopkeeper see the whole day at once,
fill the gaps and reach the cash check. Its extra tap is paid once and amortised across every
section he touches.

rails, capture has failed and the numbers are recalled rather than recorded.

06 The write path

Identical on both paths. This is what makes the two speeds safe.

STEP        BEHAVIOUR

1 · append  Save appends an immutable entry: {id, dayId, section, amount, mode?, partyId?,
            dueDate?, createdAt, source:"manual"} . Editing a line writes a new version; it does not
            mutate in place.

2 · local   The local write commits and the UI proceeds. No spinner, no optimistic-then-rollback. Per
            decision 9 the local write is the write.

3 · queue   The entry joins the sync queue. Queue depth drives SyncIndicator and nothing else.

4 · derive  All day figures -- cash received, cash paid, expected balance, net sales, section totals, progress
            -- are computed from entries on read. Nothing is cached and reconciled later. This is what
            guarantees a correction or a deletion can never leave a stale total on a screen.

5 · source Always "manual" in V1. The field exists so that decision 1 stays reversible without a migration.

The derive-on-read rule is the single most important line in this section. Storing running totals is the standard
optimisation and it is the standard way a ledger app ends up showing a number that no longer matches its own
entries.

07 Edge cases

Eight, each with a ruling. These are where a two-path design either holds together or quietly
corrupts the day.

E1 Rail tapped before the day has been started

RULING      Intercept with opening cash (T6), then continue straight into the intended form with the
NEVER       section preserved.

            Drop the user on Today after the interception. The intent was a cash sale; losing it costs the
            tap saving this whole strategy exists to protect.

E2 Rail tapped when today is already closed

RULING      Rails render disabled with a visible line -- "Today is closed. Post a correction instead." --
WHY         routing to T16.

            Decision 7 makes a closed day immutable. A disabled control that explains itself is the
            difference between a rule and a bug.

E3 Entry made into a section already marked "Nothing to enter"

RULING      The save un-skips the section. Ring recounts. A brief confirmation states it: "Expenses is no
WHY         longer marked empty."

            Decision 8 depends on skipped and empty staying distinguishable. A silent state change
            here is exactly the corruption that decision was written to prevent.

E4 Save while offline

RULING  Succeeds. Form dismisses normally. SyncIndicator shows pending and nothing else
NEVER   changes.

        A blocking dialog, a retry prompt, or a save button that goes into a loading state. A cash
        entry lost to signal is lost permanently.

E5 Back pressed mid-entry

RULING  If any digit has been typed, ConfirmSheet asks before discarding. If the form is untouched,
WHY     dismiss silently.

        Confirming an empty form trains the user to dismiss confirmations, which is how the one that
        mattered gets dismissed too.

E6 The last entry in a section is deleted

RULING  The section returns to untouched, not to skipped. The ring decrements and the cash check
WHY     re-locks if it was unlocked.

        Deleting an entry is not the same statement as "there was nothing here". Only the user can
        say the second thing, and only through NothingToEnterAction .

E7 An entry is made while the cash check is open

RULING  Permitted. The expected figure recomputes, and if the user is on the cash check screen it
WHY     invalidates any counted figure already entered and re-presents the working with a
        note that the expected amount changed.

        This is the one path that can put a wrong number in front of the shopkeeper at the exact
        moment he is deciding whether to trust the app. A stale expected balance here is worse than
        any other bug in this document.

E8 Rapid double-tap on a rail

RULING  Opening the entry form is idempotent -- one form, never two stacked. Save is debounced so
WHY     a double-tap cannot create two entries.

        One-handed use on a budget device with a laggy display makes double-taps routine, and a
        duplicated 500 sale destroys the reconciliation.

09 Acceptance criteria

Testable statements. If any of these is false, the strategy is not implemented, whatever the
screens look like.

      From a cold start on Today, the first digit of a cash sale is reachable in one tap, and the entry is
      committed in two.
      Saving from a rail returns to Today; saving from the list returns to the list. Neither ever returns to the
      other.

      The entry form is one component, instantiated with a section and an origin. No forked implementation
      exists.

      Every entry, from either path, lands in the same day record and is indistinguishable in storage from the
      other path's entries.
      No day figure shown anywhere is read from a stored total. All are derived on read.
      A save completes with the device in aeroplane mode, with no dialog and no button state change.

      Entering a line into a skipped section un-skips it and says so.
      Deleting the last line of a section returns it to untouched and re-locks the cash check.

      An entry made while the cash check is open invalidates any counted figure and re-presents the
      working.
      Double-tapping a rail produces one form; double-tapping save produces one entry.
