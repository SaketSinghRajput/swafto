SWAFTO -- Resourcing & AI Strategy

 Why we're building lean and phased in year one -- and why that is the strongest path to
 the AI-helper vision, not a detour from it

 To: Rajanikanta Garnaik, Founder & Product Owner
 From: Saket Singh, Chief Technical Associate
 Date: 23 July 2026

Purpose

 This memo lays out the resourcing and AI-infrastructure strategy for SWAFTO's first year, and the evidence
 behind it. It accompanies three companion documents already in the project folder -- the AI Infrastructure
 ADR, the Architecture Debt Prevention Plan, and the Enhanced Architecture diagram -- and pulls the
 reasoning for all three into one place, with outside evidence, so the recommendation can be evaluated on its
 merits rather than taken on faith.

Problem Statement

 SWAFTO's ambition is large: a trustworthy daily closing tool for MSME shop owners today, growing into a full
 AI helper for MSME owners, with a possible future deeptech position. The natural instinct is to build toward
 the full architecture -- private AI infrastructure, autonomous operations tooling, multi-shop support, formal
 compliance -- as early as possible. That instinct is understandable but, based on how comparable startups
 actually succeed or fail, it is the wrong sequence for a team this size at this stage. The risk is not ambition --
 it's spending scarce capital and a nine-person team's attention on infrastructure the product doesn't need yet,
 before the core hypothesis (will an MSME shop owner trust SWAFTO over their paper register?) is proven.

Goals of This Strategy

· Prove the core hypothesis -- MSME owners trust SWAFTO's daily closing over the paper register -- with

     the fastest, cheapest path that can prove it.

· Preserve capital for the moment it has the highest leverage: after real usage data exists, not before.
· Build a technical foundation that will not need to be rewritten as the company scales (multi-tenant

     schema, immutable transactions, RBAC, audit logging -- see Architecture Debt Prevention Plan).

· Keep the AI-helper and deeptech ambitions fully intact as later phases, funded by evidence rather than by

     plan.

Non-Goals for Year One

· Private LLM / GPU infrastructure now -- the real constraint on building a proprietary model is usage

     data, not compute or cash; that data only exists after real users are live. See ADR-001.

· Layer 11 (Autonomous AI Ops) -- internal engineering tooling, not customer-facing product value;

     needs real production incident history to be useful at all.
· Chasing AI feature parity -- Dukaan AI already ships free, offline, 24-language voice billing and AI bill

     scanning. Matching that feature-for-feature is not winnable with this team size, and is not where
     SWAFTO's real differentiation lives.

· Formal deeptech positioning immediately -- the ambition is legitimate and there is now a real

     government pathway for it (see Evidence, below) -- but it should be earned with real technical depth, not
     claimed before it exists.
The Evidence Base -- Why This Is Necessary, Not Just Cautious

This is not a generic "move slow" argument. It is grounded in what is currently happening in the market, in
how comparable AI and deeptech startups actually succeed or fail, and in what SWAFTO's own direct
competitors have already done.

1. The industry itself is moving toward buying, not building

The share of enterprises building their own AI systems dropped from 47% in 2024 to 24% in 2025, as mature
commercial models made in-house builds unnecessary for most use cases. Venture investors have shifted
their evaluation question from "do you use AI" to "where does your advantage live without the model" --
durability now matters more than visible AI usage. The practical playbook recommended across current
build-vs-buy guidance is exactly the one used in ADR-001: start with commercial APIs, add an abstraction
layer from day one, collect production data systematically, and evaluate fine-tuning after real usage exists.

2. Fine-tuning is cheap; training your own foundation model is not, and nobody does
the latter

Fine-tuning a small open-source model (7­14B parameters) on your own data costs roughly $500­$5,000 in
compute today, versus $78­100 million to train a frontier model from scratch. This confirms what ADR-001
already concluded: the GPU/hardware cost was never the real blocker to "owning our own model" --
proprietary data is. Buying dedicated infrastructure now doesn't shorten that timeline; only real users do.

3. Deeptech specifically punishes premature infrastructure investment

Across high-growth startup failures, premature scaling is the primary cause in roughly 74% of cases.
Deeptech ventures are especially exposed: the common failure pattern is technology-push without market
validation -- building sophisticated infrastructure before the market need is proven, then running out of
capital before proving it. Theranos is the best-known cautionary example: premature technical claims without
rigorous product validation. The lesson directly applicable to SWAFTO is sequencing, not ambition --
validate first, then invest in depth.

4. The vertical-AI companies that actually won did it in this order -- API first, proprietary
workflow data second

Harvey AI is the clearest comparable case. It is built entirely on top of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google models
via API -- it does not train its own foundation model. Its moat came from embedding directly into legal
workflows (partnering with firms like Allen & Overy) and accumulating proprietary process data that no public
dataset replicates. That combination -- hosted models plus proprietary workflow data -- took Harvey to an
$11 billion valuation and roughly $190 million in ARR within about three years. Tellingly, general-purpose
models have now caught up to specialized legal models on standard benchmarks, which means Harvey's
durable advantage was never the model -- it was the workflow data and trust built on top of it. That is
precisely the argument for treating SWAFTO's Formula/Score Engine, not the AI layer, as the real moat.

Vyapar's growing losses despite real revenue show that funding and market size do not guarantee winning;

out-featuring them is not a realistic year-one strategy for a nine-person team. Out-trusting them on the one
thing they treat as a commodity -- correctness of the daily closing -- is.

6. The deeptech ambition has a real, current path in India -- it doesn't need to be
claimed early to remain valid

In February 2026, DPIIT issued a revised national framework that formally recognizes "Deep Tech Startup"
as a distinct category for the first time, with an extended 20-year eligibility window and a raised s300 crore
turnover cap specifically to accommodate long-gestation, research-intensive ventures. This was paired with
the launch of Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0, a s10,000 crore corpus with an expanded deep tech focus.
This matters directly for SWAFTO: there is now a concrete, government-recognized designation and funding
pathway to grow into once the technical depth (proprietary vernacular NLU, models trained on real MSME
data) actually exists. Deferring the claim doesn't mean deferring the ambition -- it means qualifying for it for
real, at a moment when the policy environment is actively built to support exactly that move.

How We'll Know This Strategy Is Working

 Leading indicators (weeks)
· Pilot shop owners complete a full daily closing without abandoning mid-flow.
· Formula/Score Engine outputs match what shop owners independently verify against their own records.
· Engineering stays on the Phase 1 architecture checklist (multi-tenant schema, RBAC, audit logging,

     tested Formula Engine) without shortcuts.

 Lagging indicators (months)
· Daily-active usage retention among pilot shops beats or matches free incumbents' reported engagement.
· Cost stays within the s12L­17L Year 1 band -- a material overrun is the signal to revisit this plan, not

     silently absorb it.

· A concrete Phase 2 trigger appears (meaningful pilot data volume, or a specific customer/partner asking

     for a Phase 3 capability) before that phase is funded.
