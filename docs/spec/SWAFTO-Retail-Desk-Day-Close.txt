SWAFTO · RETAIL DESK V1 · SCREEN DESIGN IN MARIGOLD LEDGER

The sheet is the product.
No microphone.

Voice capture is out, and no model touches the input path. What
replaces it is the thing your paper Retail Desk sheet already is: a nine-
section day close that ends in a cash reconciliation. Here is that
reframed as screens, plus every output from the 15-07 note mapped to
the input that produces it -- and the six places the sheet currently can't
produce one.

      Why this makes the product stronger, not weaker

     Your own ADR-001 already concluded that "AI automation" is not a
     differentiator -- Dukaan AI ships free offline voice billing in 24
     languages including Odia. And your resource plan names the actual
     wedge: trustworthy daily cash/credit closing, a "trust/correctness
     wedge against free incumbents". Removing voice doesn't cost you the
     moat. It stops you competing on the one axis you were already going
     to lose, and puts everything behind the axis you can win: the number
     in the cash box matching the number on the screen.

05 -- BEFORE YOU BUILD

Six things the sheet can't currently answer

Each one is a decision, not a bug. They are cheap now and expensive after
four Flutter developers have written forms against V1.

01 "UPI auto-reconciliation" is doing a lot of work on that sheet

            Opening UPI, UPI sales and closing UPI are all marked auto. If that integration isn't
            live on day one, half the reconciliation is unverifiable and the app can only balance
            the cash side -- which is still valuable, but it is a different promise. Note that
            statement parsing is a data integration, not AI, so it doesn't conflict with the
            decision you just made.

                Decide: ship V1 with manual UPI totals and reconcile only cash, or block launch on
                the UPI integration? Recommend the former -- cash is where the leakage is.

02 "Shop stock" as an expense mode breaks the cash equation

            An expense settled from shop stock is a real cost but not a cash outflow. If it lands
            in the cash-paid total, every expected balance is wrong by that amount and the
            user's first reconciliation fails for a reason they can't see.

                Rule: expense modes split into cash-affecting (Cash) and non-cash-affecting (UPI,
                Other, Udhar, Shop stock). Only Cash enters the cash equation; all modes enter
                total expenses.

03 Personal withdrawal is not an expense

            It reduces cash and it must not touch profit. Your profit formula on the sheet ( Net
            sales - purchase - expense ) is right precisely because withdrawal is absent from
            it -- worth stating explicitly in the engine, because it is the single most common
            error in small-shop bookkeeping and one intern will "fix" it back.

                Rule: withdrawal reduces cash, never expenses, never profit. Cover it with a
                Formula Engine test that fails loudly.

04 Purchases have no payment term, so
          "Payment Due" can't mean overdue

            The supplier outstanding is computable, but nothing on the sheet says when it's
            owed. Without a term, the best the app can say is "23 days old" -- which is not an
            alert, it's a fact.

                Add: an optional "due in ___ days" on udhar-mode purchases, defaulting per
                supplier after the first entry.

05 Inventory pressure has no input to stand on

            Your own note marks it "(future)", correctly. "Top 3 items sold" is free text, which is
            a nice diary line and useless as data. There is no item master, no quantity, no
            stock count anywhere in V1 -- so this output cannot exist without a new input
            section, and adding one lengthens the day close, which is the one thing you
            cannot afford.

                Decide: leave it out of V1 entirely and don't show it greyed out, or introduce a
                minimal item list (name + qty sold) as an optional tenth section.

06 Nothing yet says what happens when the day isn't closed

            Shops miss days. If day 16's opening cash depends on day 15's actual count and
            day 15 was never closed, the chain breaks -- and a ledger that silently guesses is
            worse than no ledger.

                Decide: on reopening after a gap, prompt for a single cash count and mark the
                intervening days "not closed" rather than interpolating. Never invent a balance.
