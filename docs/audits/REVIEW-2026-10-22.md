### `F1` Behavioural telemetry has no consent purpose and no notice item; it cannot be bundled into account_and_ledger

**BLOCKER** · law

Document §12C asks the Behaviour Engine to measure workflow started, completed, abandoned, time-to-completion, validation failures, corrections and repeated attempts. Keyed to a user or shop id, all of it is digital personal data of an identifiable shopkeeper, so DPDP applies in full.

Two provisions bite. DPDP s.5 requires an itemised notice stating the personal data to be processed and the purpose, BEFORE processing. The notice currently lists three purposes and nothing resembling telemetry. DPDP s.6(1) requires consent to be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and — the operative words here — "limited to such personal data as is necessary for the specified purpose". Keeping a day book does not require knowing that the shopkeeper abandoned the sale form four times. So telemetry cannot ride on account_and_ledger, which is the required purpose the account rests on; bundling it would make a required consent cover unnecessary data, which is the precise defect s.6(1) is drafted against.

This also forces a product decision that D4 does not address. If telemetry is a separate optional purpose, a shopkeeper who declines it must still get a working app — and Behaviour Engine dimension C (Workflow Behaviour) then has no data. Dimensions A (Business Activity), B (Financial Behaviour) and D (Business Consistency) are different: every input they need is already a ledger entry or a closing, consented under account_and_ledger as "the entries you type". That split is the whole of the fix.

Note for scope: DPDP contains no equivalent of GDPR Article 22. There is no Indian right to object to automated decision-making or to demand an explanation of the score. Do not import one and do not build to it.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:89-93 — CONSENT_PURPOSES is exactly ['account_and_ledger','sms_reminders','crash_diagnostics']; the DB CHECK constraint at spec.ts:286 rejects any other value, so a telemetry row cannot even be written today. apps/api/src/dpdp/routes.ts:68-102 — NOTICE.items has three entries, none covering workflow events. apps/web/legal/privacy.html:135-158 — the published table has the same three rows. Grep for telemetry|analytics|workflow_started|abandon|time_to_complet across apps/ and packages/ returns no implementation (only the rate-limit comments at apps/api/src/rate-limit/limits.ts:128,137 and apps/api/src/rate-limit/memory-sql.ts:6, which concern rate-limit rules, not product telemetry). So: 0 files built, and 0 notice coverage.

**Recommendation.** Add a fourth purpose `workflow_telemetry`, required:false, and a matching notice item naming exactly what is collected ("which screens you opened, which forms you finished or left, how long an entry took") and what is not ("never an amount, a customer name or a phone number"). Because the notice changes, bump PRIVACY_NOTICE_VERSION and re-ask — the consents table already stores notice_version per row (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:283) so re-asking is expressible without a migration. Then split the Behaviour Engine at the consent boundary: dimensions A, B and D derive only from entries, days and closings and run for everyone; dimension C reads telemetry and must degrade to "not measured" when the purpose is not granted, never to a zero or a penalty. Write a test that constructs a user with workflow_telemetry withdrawn and asserts no telemetry row is written and the engine still produces A, B and D.

### `F3` A 24-month inactivity erasure is published, is not built, and is shorter than the retailer's own 72-month GST retention duty

**BLOCKER** · law

Three separate problems in one promise.

First, it is published and unimplemented. The notice served by the API and the public privacy policy both state data is erased after 24 months of inactivity. No code erases on inactivity. purgeDue selects only shops with deletion_requested_at set, so an account that simply goes quiet is kept indefinitely. A published retention statement that the software does not perform is a written misstatement to every reader and to the Data Protection Board — the same class of defect the privacy page's own header comment warns about.

Second, the project's own audit says something different: three years, with a notice first. Two documents in one repository publishing two retention periods.

Third, and this is the substantive legal problem: 24 months is shorter than the duty the retailer himself is under. CGST s.36 requires a registered person to retain the records required by s.35(1) until the expiry of seventy-two months from the due date of furnishing the annual return for the year to which those records pertain (and, where an appeal or revision is pending, for one year after its final disposal, whichever is later). shops.gstin exists precisely because some SWAFTO shopkeepers are GST-registered. For those shopkeepers, an automatic erasure at month 24 destroys the digital copy of records they are statutorily obliged to be able to produce for 72 months. DPDP does not require that erasure — s.8(7) erases "as soon as it is reasonable to assume that the purpose ... is no longer being served", which is not the same as a 24-month clock, and the section expressly yields where retention is necessary for compliance with a law.

I could not verify the DPDP Rules 2025 Third Schedule thresholds against the gazetted text in this session. From the Rules as reported, the prescribed erasure periods apply to specified classes — e-commerce entities, online gaming intermediaries and social media intermediaries above large registered-user thresholds. SWAFTO is none of those classes and is nowhere near any such threshold, so no Rule-prescribed period compels a 24-month timer. Conservative reading: the 24 months is self-imposed, not required, and should be removed rather than defended.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/dpdp/routes.ts:80 — retention: 'kept while the account is in use; erased within 90 days of a deletion request, or of 24 months of inactivity'. apps/web/legal/privacy.html:141 — the identical sentence, published. docs/audits/LEGAL-AUDIT.md:139 — 'account inactive 3 years -> notice -> erasure. Needs a business decision on the exact period.' A grep for inactiv across apps/, packages/ and docs/audits/ returns exactly these three lines and nothing in apps/api/src/jobs/ — so the promise has no implementation. apps/api/src/jobs/purge-due.ts:39-45 with apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:435-439 — shopsDueForPurge filters on deletion_requested_at IS NOT NULL, i.e. requested deletions only. docs/audits/LEGAL-AUDIT.md:52 — shops.gstin exists for accountant exports.

**Recommendation.** Remove the fixed inactivity period from the notice and the policy and replace it with the honest, DPDP-compliant form: data is kept while the account is in use; after a long period of inactivity SWAFTO writes to the shopkeeper, offers an export, and erases only if there is no response. Then build it: an inactivity-notice job and an inactivity-erasure job, both with the same safe-to-run-twice shape as purge-due.ts. Whatever period is chosen must be a stated decision, must be at least 72 months plus a margin for any shop with a non-null gstin, and must be the same number in the notice, the policy and the audit. Do not ship a notice containing a period that no job enforces.

### `F4` DPDP erasure does not conflict with statutory bookkeeping retention — do not build a retention hold that refuses erasure

**HIGH** · decision-needed

This was named the single most important legal question in the brief, so here is the resolution rather than a hedge.

DPDP s.8(7) obliges the Data Fiduciary to erase personal data on withdrawal of consent or once the purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, "unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law for the time being in force". The carve-out means DPDP erasure can never force a fiduciary to destroy what a law requires it to keep. So there is no head-on conflict in the statute.

But the carve-out does not apply to SWAFTO, because the retention duty is not SWAFTO's. CGST s.35(1) and s.36 impose record-keeping and 72-month retention on the registered person — the retailer. Income Tax Act s.44AA(2) imposes the duty to keep books on the person carrying on the business — the retailer. SWAFTO is a day book the retailer chooses to type into; the repository's own documents already say it is not a tax product and produces no tax document. SWAFTO is not the statutory system of record, so it has no law requiring it to retain, so s.8(7) gives it no ground to refuse an erasure request.

The tempting wrong build is a statutory-retention hold: refuse or defer erasure for GST-registered shops on the grounds that the law needs the records. That would be SWAFTO claiming someone else's duty as its own excuse not to honour a DPDP right, and it would be wrong on s.8(7) in both directions.

Separately: s.44AA(2)'s thresholds are income exceeding ₹1,20,000 or turnover/gross receipts exceeding ₹10,00,000 in any of the three preceding years, with a proviso raising those to ₹2,50,000 and ₹25,00,000 for an individual or HUF. On the retention period for a business I must state uncertainty plainly: Rule 6F prescribes the books and Rule 6F(5) the six-year retention for the specified professions under s.44AA(1), not for a retail business under s.44AA(2), for which no retention period is expressly prescribed and the practical driver is the reassessment window under s.149. Conservative reading: treat six years from the end of the relevant assessment year as the floor, which in any case is subsumed by CGST s.36's 72 months for a registered retailer.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/dpdp/routes.ts:248-287 — the erasure route, which records a request, states liveDataErased: false, and promises erasure at +30 days and backups within 90. apps/web/legal/terms.html:94 — 'a tax product — it calculates no GST and produces no tax document'. apps/web/legal/terms.html:99-102 — section 2, 'Tax is yours', assigning tax compliance to the retailer. apps/web/legal/privacy.html:230-234 — 'This is not a tax service'. docs/audits/LEGAL-AUDIT.md:52 — gstin is optional and for accountant exports only. Statutes cited from text, not from a file in this repo: DPDP Act 2023 s.8(7); CGST Act 2017 s.35(1), s.36; Income Tax Act 1961 s.44AA(2) and its proviso; Income Tax Rules 1962 Rule 6F(5) (specified professions).

**Recommendation.** Record the decision in LEGAL-AUDIT §1.6 in these terms: erasure always proceeds; SWAFTO claims no statutory retention basis; the retailer's own duty survives the deletion and is his. Then make the deletion flow carry that fact rather than leaving it to a policy page — on the erase screen and in the confirmation SMS/email copy, before the typed DELETE: 'Your GST and income-tax records are still your responsibility after this. Download your book first.' Make the export offer a step in the flow, not a link elsewhere. Add one test that the erase screen cannot be completed without the export having been offered. Explicitly do not implement any GSTIN-conditional retention hold.

### `F5` Document §7 contradicts §9 on customer phone capture; DPDP s.6(1) necessity resolves it in favour of §9

**HIGH** · law

Document §7 lists 'Supplier/Party/Customer Name & Phone No' as a Return/Refund field. Document §9 says customer information stays optional and becomes necessary only where the business event requires identification — Credit/Udhar and Party/Supplier payments. Both cannot be built.

DPDP s.6(1) limits consent to such personal data as is necessary for the specified purpose. A cash refund across a counter identifies nobody and needs nobody identified: the money leaves the drawer and the transaction is closed. Offering a phone-number field there is collection beyond necessity, and the DPDP Rules require the notice to be itemised and specific, so a field whose necessity cannot be stated is a field that should not be offered.

The four settlement states split cleanly on exactly this line, which is the useful part: Returned + Refund paid and Returned + Replacement are closed events requiring no identification; Returned + Credit issued and Returned + Pending settlement create a continuing receivable or payable against a named person, and that person must be identifiable or the ledger is meaningless. So necessity is determined by the settlement state, not by the workflow.

One measured mitigation worth stating: Exchange and Return/Refund introduce no new CATEGORY of personal data. The parties table already holds name (NOT NULL) and phone (nullable). What is new is the number of screens on which those optional fields are offered, which is a necessity question rather than a notice-coverage question. The existing notice item already covers 'customer and supplier names you enter'.

### `F6` Return/Refund must not name its artefact a credit note or debit note; the naming check that would stop it has no such term

**HIGH** · law

'Credit note' and 'debit note' are not descriptive English in Indian retail; they are defined instruments. CGST s.34(1) provides that where a tax invoice has been issued and the taxable value or tax charged exceeds what was payable, or the goods are returned, or the supply is found deficient, the registered person who made the supply may issue a credit note; s.34(3) provides for a debit note where the value was understated. CGST Rule 53 prescribes the particulars. s.34(2) requires the credit note to be declared in the return for the month of issue and not later than 30 November following the end of the financial year of the supply (or the annual return, whichever is earlier), and its proviso denies the reduction in output tax liability where the incidence of tax has been passed on.

For the target user the answer to the brief's question is: no document obligation. An unregistered retailer issues no tax invoice, so s.34 has nothing to attach to — there is no invoice to credit. A composition dealer under s.10 issues a bill of supply under Rule 49, not a tax invoice, so the same holds. The threshold at which this changes is GST registration under s.22(1) as raised by Notification No. 10/2019-Central Tax: aggregate turnover of ₹40 lakh for a person engaged exclusively in the supply of goods in a normal-category state (₹20 lakh in the specified special-category states), and ₹20 lakh for services. Odisha is a normal-category state for goods. Above that, the retailer's obligations are his own and are discharged in his GST software, not in SWAFTO.

So the exposure is purely one of naming, and it is the exact defect LEGAL-AUDIT §7 and check:naming already exist to prevent for 'invoice'. A Return/Refund screen whose output is labelled 'credit note' tells a shopkeeper he is holding a GST document with Rule 53 particulars, none of which SWAFTO captures. The FORBIDDEN list has seven terms and neither of these two is among them, so nothing stops it being typed into the new workflow.

**Evidence.** tools/check-export-naming.mjs:85-92 — the FORBIDDEN list: 'invoice', 'tax invoice', 'gst return', 'gstr', 'filing', 'tax document' and one further entry; no 'credit note', no 'debit note'. tools/check-export-naming.mjs:5-9 — the file's own reasoning for 'invoice' (Rule 46 particulars SWAFTO does not capture), which transfers to Rule 53 unchanged. docs/audits/LEGAL-AUDIT.md:304-328 — LEGAL-AUDIT §7, the source the FORBIDDEN list is transcribed from, which also lacks the two terms. tools/check-export-naming.mjs:170 — matching is word-bounded, so adding a two-word term needs care. **Recommendation.** Add ['credit note', 'debit note'] to FORBIDDEN in tools/check-export-naming.mjs with the Rule 53 reason written in, add the matching rows to LEGAL-AUDIT §7's may/may-not table so the check stays transcribed rather than invented, and run npm run prove:verifiers because the check changed. Name the four settlement states in neutral shop language that cannot be mistaken for a tax instrument: 'refund paid', 'shop credit given', 'item replaced', 'not settled yet' — and the Odia and Hindi equivalents, which the Latin-script check cannot police and which stay H13.

### `F7` What SWAFTO may say about a UPI transaction: a settlement claim it has no standing to make, and nothing currently stops the words appearing

**HIGH** · law

Document §2 asks for this and is right, and D3 has already fixed the substance. What is missing is the enforcement.

The legal footing: the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 s.4(1) provides that no person other than the Reserve Bank shall commence or operate a payment system except under an authorisation issued by the Bank. Access to the status of a UPI transaction belongs to the participants in that system — the PSP banks, and third-party application providers operating under a sponsor PSP bank per NPCI's UPI procedural guidelines. SWAFTO is neither, holds no VPA, routes no funds and has no integration. It therefore has no means of knowing whether a UPI transaction succeeded, and no standing to represent that it does.

This is not merely imprecise. 'UPI verified by SWAFTO' is a representation to a person who is, in all likelihood, a consumer for the purposes of the Consumer Protection Act 2019 (see F8 on the s.2(7) livelihood explanation), about a fact SWAFTO cannot establish, on which the shopkeeper would reasonably rely to close his day. That is squarely in unfair-trade-practice territory under s.2(47), and it is the worse exposure, because it costs the shopkeeper real money when a payment he believed verified never arrived.

The good news, measured: the words are not there yet. There are zero occurrences of 'verif' in the shipped UI string table. The whole risk is ahead of the build, which is the cheapest moment to install a guard.

### `F8` The three scores: CICRA exposure is manageable and conditional, but the live risk is Consumer Protection Act misrepresentation, which makes D4's validation condition a legal control

**HIGH** · law

Taking the two regimes separately, conservatively.

Credit information. The Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act 2005 requires registration with the Reserve Bank to carry on the business of a credit information company, which means collecting, processing and providing credit information to specified users who are its members. A score computed inside the shopkeeper's own account, from his own entries, shown only to him, and disclosed to nobody, is not that business. So the three scores are outside CICRA — but the finding is conditional, and the condition is the whole of it: the moment any score, band or derived signal is disclosed to a lender, an NBFC, a marketplace or any third party, or is used to route the shopkeeper towards credit, the character changes and CICRA, the RBI Digital Lending framework for lending service providers, and the Account Aggregator framework all come into view. LEGAL-AUDIT §8 already lists lending and data-sharing as triggers; it does not list computing and displaying a creditworthiness-like score, which is the step that makes the sharing feature look obvious and harmless.

Consumer protection, which is the live one. A sole-proprietor shopkeeper paying for SWAFTO is very likely a 'consumer' under CPA 2019 s.2(7): the exclusion for a commercial purpose carries an explanation that commercial purpose does not include use by a person of goods bought and used exclusively for earning a livelihood by means of self-employment. So SWAFTO's representations to him are representations to a consumer. A number labelled 'Business Health', presented as a measurement, computed by a formula whose mathematics is not validated, is a representation about the quality of a service under s.2(47) and is reachable by the Central Consumer Protection Authority under s.18 and s.21. That is what makes D4's condition — mathematics defined and validated in the docs before production use — a legal control rather than engineering hygiene.

Document §14's rule that no score may punish a small business has the same footing and no dedicated statute: a score that marks a ₹5,000/day shop unhealthy for being small is not measuring health, and presenting it as if it were is the misrepresentation. Scale-relative benchmarking is the remedy.

To be explicit where a GDPR habit would mislead: DPDP has no automated-decision-making provision. There is no Indian right to contest the score or demand its logic. The restraint is CPA and the product's own rule that the owner keeps decision authority, not DPDP.

### `F9` The e-commerce rules do not attach, and the one step that would change that is a message to the customer — currently zero

**MEDIUM** · law

Answering the brief's question directly: recording a return or refund on the shopkeeper's behalf creates no obligation on SWAFTO under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 or the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020.

The reason is definitional. 'E-commerce' under s.2(16) is the buying or selling of goods or services, including digital products, over a digital or electronic network, and an 'e-commerce entity' under s.2(17) is a person who owns, operates or manages a digital or electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce. SWAFTO operates no such facility. The sale happened across a physical counter, between two people, and was complete before SWAFTO heard of it; the app records what already occurred, which is the paper sheet's role. It is neither marketplace nor inventory model, lists no goods, takes no order, and — per LEGAL-AUDIT §8 — moves no money. So the Rules' obligations on refund timelines, grievance officers for consumer complaints and seller disclosure do not attach, and the duty to the returning customer remains the retailer's alone.

The trigger that would change this is narrow and worth naming before it is proposed, because it will be: SWAFTO messaging the customer. The moment the Return/Refund workflow sends an SMS to a customer — 'your shop credit of ₹400 is recorded', or a refund confirmation — SWAFTO acquires a direct processing relationship with a person who is not its user and has given it no consent, triggering a DPDP s.5 notice obligation to a non-user data principal, and a TCCCPR 2018 / TRAI DLT consent and template problem on top. Measured: this does not exist. There are exactly two SMS templates in the product and both go to the account holder, and apps/api/src/sms/port.ts refuses any unregistered templateId, so a customer-facing message cannot be sent by accident — only by someone adding a template.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/auth/routes.ts:145 — templateId 'otp_signin'. apps/api/src/dpdp/routes.ts:368 — templateId 'otp_account_deletion'. These are the only two templateId call sites in apps/api/src outside the port and stub. apps/api/src/sms/port.ts:47-50 — an unregistered templateId throws, so the template registry is a real gate. docs/audits/LEGAL-AUDIT.md:334-346 — the four grounds SWAFTO handles no funds and is a bookkeeping record. apps/web/legal/terms.html:106-113 — Terms section 3, placing the customer record with the retailer. Statutes cited from text: Consumer Protection Act 2019 s.2(16), s.2(17); Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020; TCCCPR 2018 and the TRAI DLT framework.

**Recommendation.** Record the conclusion in LEGAL-AUDIT as a new short section — not an e-commerce entity, with the s.2(16)/(17) reasoning — so that nobody re-opens it, and add one row to the §8 trigger table: 'Sending any message to the shopkeeper's customer | DPDP s.5 notice to a non-user data principal; TCCCPR 2018 / DLT consent; and it starts to look like operating a facility for the transaction'. Keep the customer-facing template count at zero in V1. If a customer notification is ever wanted, the lawful shape is the shopkeeper sending it from his own handset through his own messaging app, with SWAFTO composing text and never transmitting — which also keeps the phone number off the server path entirely.

### `F10` The two published legal documents take different positions on who is the Data Fiduciary for customer records, and the Terms do not contain the s.8(2) processing clause they would need

**MEDIUM** · law

The Terms assert it: when the retailer records a customer's name or phone, he is the Data Fiduciary for that record, with the duties that follow. The privacy policy hedges the same question, saying the tools work 'whichever way the analysis falls'. Two live documents, one assertion and one hedge, on the question that determines who answers a customer's erasure request. The new workflows raise the stakes, because Exchange and Return/Refund multiply the screens on which a third party's phone number can be typed.

The Terms' position is defensible under the Act. DPDP s.2 defines a Data Fiduciary as one who alone or with others determines the purpose and means of processing, and separately defines a Data Processor as one who processes on behalf of a Data Fiduciary. A retailer deciding to write a customer's number in his book determines the purpose; SWAFTO stores it on his instruction. So retailer-as-fiduciary, SWAFTO-as-processor is coherent.

But it carries a consequence the Terms have not discharged. s.8(2) permits a Data Fiduciary to engage a Data Processor only under a valid contract. If the retailer is the fiduciary, the retailer needs that contract with SWAFTO — and the only instrument between them is these Terms, which SWAFTO writes. At present section 3 allocates responsibility without containing processing terms: no statement that SWAFTO processes customer data only on the retailer's instruction and for no purpose of its own, no security undertaking, no return-or-erase-on-termination term, no limit on sub-processors. A clause that assigns the duty to the retailer while omitting the contract that duty requires leaves the retailer in breach of s.8(2) by using the product.

There is a second, sharper reason to settle this now: if SWAFTO is a processor for customer data, then feeding customer-linked data into the Behaviour Engine or the three scores — anything that is SWAFTO's own purpose rather than the retailer's instruction — would make SWAFTO a fiduciary for that data in its own right, with its own notice and consent duties to people who are not its users and whom it cannot reach. The engine's inputs must therefore be the shopkeeper's own activity and his aggregate figures, never a customer's identity.

**Evidence.** apps/web/legal/terms.html:106-113 — Terms section 3, 'Your customers' details are your responsibility', asserting the retailer is the Data Fiduciary and listing the three tools (optional phone, per-customer export, removal). apps/web/legal/privacy.html:176-184 — the privacy policy's version of the same paragraph, which says 'whichever way the analysis falls' and does not commit. apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:418-425 — redactParty, the removal tool the clause promises. packages/schema/src/spec.ts:189-197 — parties.phone nullable, the optionality the clause promises. Statutes cited from text: DPDP Act 2023 s.2 (Data Fiduciary, Data Processor), s.8(2).

**Recommendation.** Settle on retailer-as-fiduciary, SWAFTO-as-processor, make the privacy policy say the same thing as the Terms rather than hedging, and add the s.8(2) processing terms to the Terms: SWAFTO processes customer records only on the retailer's instruction and for no purpose of its own; the security measures it applies; no sub-processor outside ap-south-1; and what happens to customer records on termination. Then enforce the boundary in code rather than in prose — the Behaviour Engine and the three scores must take party_id counts and aggregate amounts and never a name or a phone number, which is a testable property: assert that no engine input type carries a name or phone field. That also keeps B36's existing promise (no customer name or phone in diagnostics) true of the new subsystem by construction.

### `F11` Behavioural telemetry must die with the account, and there may be a DPDP Rules 6 log-retention duty pulling the other way — flagged with the uncertainty stated

**MEDIUM** · risk

Answering the brief's third question on telemetry: yes, it is personal data, and no, it does not survive an erasure request.

Workflow events keyed to a user or shop are personal data of an identifiable person, so DPDP s.8(7) erasure reaches them, as do the s.11 access right and the export (see F2). The derived values are the subtler half: a stored Business Health score, a stored Cash Confidence number, a stored 'abandoned 4 of 7 sales forms' aggregate are all computed about one identifiable shopkeeper and are therefore his personal data too. They must be erased, not merely orphaned. The repo's own 'no stored totals, everything derived on read' rule happens to make this easy — if the scores are never persisted, there is nothing extra to erase, and the erasure of entries erases the scores by construction. That is the lazy correct answer and it should be the design.

The counter-pull, with uncertainty stated honestly: the DPDP Rules 2025 security-safeguards rule is reported to require a Data Fiduciary to maintain logs and monitoring of access to personal data and to retain them for a period of one year. I could not verify that against the gazetted text in this session, and I will not assert the exact sub-rule or period. If it is correct, it is the one retention duty in this whole analysis that falls on SWAFTO rather than on the retailer, and it means security access logs are not erased on an erasure request — they are retained under the s.8(7) 'necessary for compliance with any law' carve-out, which does properly apply here.

The practical consequence is that two kinds of log must not be built as one table. Product telemetry (which form the shopkeeper abandoned) is consent-based, optional, and erased. Security access logs (who read which personal data, when) are legally mandated, not consent-based, and retained. Merge them and the merged table is both un-erasable and un-withdrawable, which fails both duties at once.

**Evidence.** CLAUDE.md:61-64 — 'No stored totals. Everything is derived on read', the rule that makes score erasure free if followed. apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:444-481 — purgeShop, the transaction that would need every new telemetry table added to it, and which already diverges from exportUser (F2). packages/schema/src/spec.ts:336-347 — rate_events, which already shows this repo separating a security-operational store from personal data deliberately (the comment notes the table would otherwise become a list of every phone number that ever tried to sign in, a DPDP problem). Statutes: DPDP Act 2023 s.8(7), s.11, cited from text. DPDP Rules 2025 security-safeguards log retention: UNVERIFIED in this session; not read from a primary source, sub-rule and period not asserted.

**Recommendation.** Do not persist any score or derived aggregate; compute all three scores on read from entries, days and closings, which satisfies erasure for free and matches the repo's existing no-stored-totals rule. Keep product telemetry in its own table under the workflow_telemetry purpose from F1, add it to purgeShop and to exportUser together (which the F2 check will then enforce), and make withdrawal of that purpose delete the rows rather than merely stop collection — withdrawal as easy as giving, per the existing /v1/me/consent design. Keep any security access log in a separate table, outside the consent model, and before building it have someone read the gazetted DPDP Rules 2025 security-safeguards rule and record the actual sub-rule number and retention period in LEGAL-AUDIT — this is a human action, not something to settle on inference, and it belongs in docs/handover/HUMAN-ACTIONS.md.

### `UPI-4` A UPI opening balance is not needed: under confirm-receipts, opening and all outflows cancel and the residual IS the unconfirmed set

**HIGH** · requirement

The argument, from the decision itself. Actual UPI is defined as the sum of UPI receipts the shopkeeper confirms — not a bank balance. So both sides of U4 are built from the same entry set:

  Expected UPI Closing = OpeningUPI + Σ(all upi inflow entries) − Σ(all upi outflow entries)
  Actual UPI Closing   = OpeningUPI + Σ(confirmed upi inflow entries) − Σ(all upi outflow entries)

The shopkeeper never confirms an outflow against anything — he authored it and there is no counterparty screen to check it against. So the outflow sum is identical on both sides, and OpeningUPI is identical on both sides. Subtract:

  UPI Difference = − Σ(unconfirmed upi inflow entries)

Opening UPI cancels exactly. It cannot ever change the difference by one paisa. Carrying it would add a column to `days`, a migration, a carry-forward rule, a D6-style "opening is yesterday's actual, never yesterday's expected" invariant, and a new way for the chain to break — in exchange for a term that provably contributes nothing. The ledger is per-day and self-contained.

Two consequences worth stating plainly. First, the difference is always ≤ 0 under this model: it can show a receipt he cannot find, never a receipt he did not record (see UPI-6). Second, "UPI Difference = 0" — which the review document requires for a clean close (pdftotext.txt:122,131) — is achievable here, and is exactly what CLAUDE.md:129-132 says is unachievable against a bank balance. The decision is self-consistent; it is the arithmetic that collapses.

**Evidence.** Decision 3, CLAUDE.md:129-132. Outflow modes from input.ts:132,153,178,196. The cancellation is algebraic, not measured. Contrast with the cash side, where opening genuinely does not cancel because the actual figure is an independent physical count: `captureCountedCash` (input.ts:211-217) and `dailyCashHealth` (operational.ts:80-115) take a number from the drawer, not from the entries.

**Recommendation.** Do not add `opening_upi` to `DayRecord` or to the `days` table. State the per-day scope in the `upi.ts` header the way primary.ts states D2 and D3, because the next developer will try to add it. Then reframe the screen to match the arithmetic: not two balances to count, but a list — "7 UPI receipts, ₹3,120. Open your payment app and tick the ones you can see." — with the residual shown underneath. Presenting a derived checklist as an independent reconciliation is the "false certainty" the review document's section 2 is about (pdftotext.txt:37-53), applied to the arithmetic rather than to the wording.

### `UPI-5` An unconfirmed receipt needs three states, not two — or a lazy day and a real loss produce the same number

**HIGH** · requirement

If a receipt is either confirmed or not, then a day where the shopkeeper never opened his payment app and a day where ₹500 genuinely never arrived both yield the same UPI difference. The first is an incomplete workflow; the second is money gone. Treating them alike makes the difference uninterpretable and makes the Cash Confidence score unearnable, in the same way a free-form adjustment would (decision 2's reasoning, CLAUDE.md:125-128).

Three states:
  not_checked   — default. He has not looked. Not a discrepancy. This is what the review document's "required transactions completed" clause covers (pdftotext.txt:133): it should hold the clean-close path shut without producing a difference.
  confirmed     — he saw it in his payment app.
  not_found     — he looked and it is not there. This is the real signal, and the only one that should produce a UPI discrepancy record.

The UPI difference is then Σ(not_found), and the count of `not_checked` is reported separately as completeness, never folded into money. This mirrors D8's existing distinction between a skipped section and an untouched section, which already produce the same total and a different day state (types.ts:97-101, `skippedSections`) — the same problem, already solved once in this codebase.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:95-101 (D8 skipped-vs-untouched precedent on `DayRecord.skippedSections`); review document requirement at scratchpad/pdf/pdftotext.txt:133 ("required transactions completed"); decision 2 reasoning at CLAUDE.md:125-128.

**Recommendation.** Model confirmation as a per-entry enum with `not_checked` as the default, append-only like everything else (D1) — a confirmation is a new row referencing the entry, never a mutation of it. Reuse the `skippedSections` shape rather than inventing a second vocabulary. One vector per state transition, and one that proves a day with 7 `not_checked` receipts has a UPI difference of 0 and a completeness of 0/7, not a difference of −3120.

### `UPI-6` A receipt in the bank but never entered in SWAFTO cannot be represented, and should not be — one typed adjustment row is the whole answer

**MEDIUM** · decision-needed

Can it be represented? No, and the schema is right to refuse. Every money fact in this system is an `entries` row with a `section` and a `mode` (migration 0001, `entries_source_ck` and the section/mode CHECKs), and entries are append-only (D1). There is no shape for "money I know about but cannot attribute". Under the confirm-receipts model the difference is structurally ≤ 0 (UPI-4), so a surplus cannot even be expressed as a difference.

Should it be? No, not directly. A row the system creates to absorb an unexplained credit is a figure nobody typed, which is the one thing CLAUDE.md:72-76 forbids outright. But the shopkeeper typing it himself is not inference — it is data entry, and it is the correct path. He opens his payment app, sees ₹500 he did not record, and almost always knows what it was: a sale he forgot to enter. He enters the sale. The loop the review document demands (activity → traceable transaction) is preserved exactly.

For the residual case where he genuinely cannot identify it, decision 2 already supplies the mechanism: the fixed adjustment list needs one row, worded as what it is — "UPI receipt found in my payment app, not in SWAFTO". Typed, so it cannot absorb an arbitrary discrepancy; attributable, so the Behaviour Engine can count how often it fires, which is itself a signal about entry discipline.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/migrations/0001_init.postgres.up.sql:81-84 (`entries.source` CHECK `('manual','imported')` — the `imported` value exists precisely to keep the statement-parsing door open, per docs/spec/source/SWAFTO-Retail-Desk-Decisions.txt:36-38); D1 append-only and the no-inference rule at CLAUDE.md:52-54, 72-76; decision 2 at CLAUDE.md:125-128.

**Recommendation.** Add exactly one typed adjustment row for it, and make the primary call-to-action "enter the sale" with the adjustment as the fallback, not the default. Do not add a UPI surplus concept. Note that `entries.source` already carries `'manual' | 'imported'` for every row — that field was put in from the first commit specifically so an imported UPI figure can never be mistaken for a typed one, and it is the correct place to record provenance if statement import ever lands. ### `UPI-7` The paper sheet has Opening-UPI and closing-UPI lines, but no UPI reconciliation step — and the sheet marked those lines "auto"

**MEDIUM** · risk

### `HB-01` The front door has no ACT zone, and the entry forms are dead ends: a shopkeeper cannot record the first sale of the day

**BLOCKER** · bug

Document §10 wants three zones on the home page (ACT / KNOW / COMPLETE). What is built is KNOW plus one COMPLETE button. T1 renders one row per section, filtered to sections that already have entries, and its footer holds exactly two buttons: 'Close the day' and the privacy link. So the only way to reach an entry form from Today is to tap a section that is ALREADY non-empty. On the first morning of any business day there are zero entries, therefore zero rows, therefore zero routes to T7/T8/T9/T10. The app's own canonical empty-state copy ('Nothing entered yet today. Start with a sale.') names an action the screen does not offer. Worse, navigation is one-way: there is no back handler, no bottom nav, and no production code path that returns to T1 from T7-T10 (only confirmOpeningCash and the consent flow set screen:'T1'). T5 (the nine-section day-close list) and T6 (opening cash — the first screen of a shop's life) have NO production caller at all; every goTo('T5')/goTo('T6') in the tree is in a test. Human behaviour makes this fatal rather than cosmetic: the first thing that happens in a shop is a customer, not a report, and the second thing is wanting to look at the day and then go back. A shopkeeper who taps 'Cash sales' to record a sale and lands in a form he cannot leave will close the app, and the app will have lost the day.

**Evidence.** apps/mobile/src/screens/T1.tsx:29-38 (SUMMARY_SECTIONS), T1.tsx:164 (`.filter((row) => row.count > 0)`), T1.tsx:95-150 (footer = t1-close-day + t1-privacy only); apps/mobile/src/app/App.tsx:202-212 (T1's only callbacks: goTo('T11'), onOpenPrivacy, onOpenSection); apps/mobile/src/app/App.tsx:140 (T5's onOpenSection, but nothing navigates TO T5); `grep -rn "goTo(" apps/mobile/src` yields only App.tsx:140, 208, 210 — no goTo('T5'), no goTo('T6'), no goTo('T1'); `grep -rn "BackHandler|onBack|navigate" apps/mobile/src` yields 0 hits; apps/mobile/src/app/session.tsx:327 and :383 are the only two assignments of screen:'T1'.

**Recommendation.** Put the ACT zone on T1 as fixed buttons that exist whether or not the section has entries — Sale, Expense, Purchase, Credit, Payment — and stop deriving navigation from data presence. Add one unconditional way back to T1 from every screen (a header affordance plus Android hardware back). Give T5 and T6 real entry points: T6 on first run and after a gap, T5 as the step before T11 so 'Close the day' walks the nine sections instead of jumping to the cash count. Add a navigation test that asserts, from a store with zero entries, that every committed screen is reachable and that T1 is reachable from every screen — the current screen tests all start by calling goTo() directly, which is why this is invisible to them.

### `HB-02` The UPI ledger the document and decision D3 require does not exist: 0 files, 0 columns, 0 screens

**BLOCKER** · gap

Document §4 demands a UPI expected-vs-actual with its own difference alongside the cash one, and the 2026-10-22 decision 3 fixes what 'actual UPI' means (the sum of UPI receipts the shopkeeper confirms). Nothing of this is built. There is no expected-UPI figure in the formula engine, no UPI counterpart to T11/T12, and the days table carries only opening_cash, counted_cash and difference_reason — there is no counted_upi column, so a UPI close has nowhere to be recorded. UPI sales are recorded as entries and correctly excluded from the cash equation (D2), and that is all. On the human side this is the dimension's hardest question and it is unanswered: confirming receipts one by one scales with the shop's UPI volume, and a festival-day retailer with 60-80 UPI receipts will not tap through 60 confirmations at 10pm after counting notes. I am reasoning here rather than citing: the repo contains no measurement of UPI receipt counts per day, so the tolerable number is unknown and must not be invented. What IS certain from the code is that there is no state in which 'he stopped confirming' can be represented — the day is open or closed, and a close requires only a cash count.

**Evidence.** `grep -rln "expectedUpi|upiDifference|upi_expected|upiActual" packages apps` → 0 files; packages/formula-engine/src/primary.ts:115-152 (the expected-cash working lines are cash-mode only) and :154-162 (netSales is the only place UPI sales are summed); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:204-220 (CREATE TABLE days has opening_cash, counted_cash, difference_reason, closed_at and no UPI column); apps/mobile/src/screens/T11.tsx:51 (`store.expectedCash()` is the only expectation on the screen) and T12.tsx:60-67 (one difference); apps/mobile/src/app/session.tsx:258-285 (confirmCount takes one figure).

**Recommendation.** Design the UPI check total-first, not receipt-first: show the expected UPI total, take one confirmed total from the shopkeeper, and only if the two disagree open the receipt list for a per-receipt tick. That makes the common day two taps and the abnormal day as long as it needs to be. Add an explicit third day outcome, 'UPI not confirmed', that is recorded as a fact and is neither a discrepancy nor a penalty — a shopkeeper who stops confirming must be able to close his cash day honestly. Before any Cash Confidence mathematics is fixed, instrument UPI receipts per day on the pilot shops and publish the measured distribution; the tolerance threshold must come from that number, not from this document.

### `HB-03` The discrepancy reason is free text that one character satisfies, so Cash Confidence will measure compliance rather than accuracy

**HIGH** · risk

The document asks that on a difference the user 'confirms truth' and a discrepancy is recorded. As built, the gate is Boolean(reason.trim()) in the screen, !differenceReason in the store, and an unconstrained TEXT column in the schema. Two canned reasons are offered ('Gave change from my pocket', 'Forgot to write something') and a free-text box sits under them. Now the human case the dimension asks about: ₹20 short at 11pm, shutter half down, a child waiting. The shopkeeper does not investigate ₹20 — the labour of finding it costs more than the money. He taps the first chip, or types one character, and the day closes. The chip he taps is the first one, which means the stored corpus of reasons will be dominated by whichever reason was listed first, and that is an artifact of ordering, not a fact about his shop. Any score computed from 'a reason was present' is then a measure of whether he tapped, i.e. compliance; and any pattern output built on the reason text is built on noise. This is the mechanism by which a Cash Confidence score becomes confidently wrong. Note also that the session layer takes `counted ?? 0`, so a code path that reaches confirmCount without a typed figure records a count of zero and a difference equal to the whole day's expected cash — the T11 button is disabled, so the UI blocks it today, but the invariant is held only by the button.

**Evidence.** apps/mobile/src/screens/T12.tsx:66 (`const canClose = difference === 0 || Boolean(reason && reason.trim())`), T12.tsx:29 (two reasons), T12.tsx:~160-190 (free-text TextInput t12-reason-text); apps/mobile/src/store/index.ts:329 (`if (countedCash !== expected && !differenceReason)`); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:210 (`difference_reason  text` with no CHECK); apps/mobile/src/app/session.tsx:261 and :275 (`counted ?? 0`); T12.tsx:16-20 records that PRODUCT-REVIEW recommendation 1 already argues for an honest 'I am not sure' and that the decision was not taken.

**Recommendation.** Type the discrepancy reason from a fixed list, for the same reason decision 2 typed adjustments — a free-form reason absorbs any gap and makes expected-vs-actual unfalsifiable. Include 'I do not know' as a first-class option and treat it as the honest answer, never a penalty; take recommendation 1's decision now rather than carrying it. Randomise or alphabetise nothing — order the list by what is actually common once there is data, and record which option was pre-selected (none) so the ordering bias is auditable. Define Cash Confidence over the reason TYPE and the magnitude distribution of differences, never over the presence of a reason, and give it a shopkeeper-set tolerance band below which a difference is recorded but does not move the score. Replace `counted ?? 0` with an explicit refusal so the zero-count invariant is not held by a disabled button.

### `HB-04` Nobody knows who is holding the phone: the app lock is an orphan constant, and one shop has exactly one user row

**HIGH** · bug

D9 ('one shop, one phone') is a statement about concurrent editing, and the build is explicit that it does not mean two devices cannot exist. What it silently also assumes is one PAIR OF HANDS, and in a real shop that is false: during a rush the phone is held by whoever is free — the owner, a son, a hired boy — and at 4pm the owner may be at the bank while the shop still sells. The data model has no room for this. There is one users row per shop (phone UNIQUE, shop_id FK) and entries carry no actor at all. Two consequences. First, the Behaviour Engine's Workflow dimension (document §12C: started / completed / abandoned / time to completion / corrections / repeated attempts) will attribute a hired boy's fumbling to the owner and then show the owner a pattern about himself that is about somebody else — and §13's Level 4/5 outputs will be advice aimed at the wrong person. Second, there is an access-control hole: APP_LOCK_PIN_DIGITS is declared with a careful comment explaining why it is four digits, and it is referenced nowhere else in the tree. There is no lock screen, no PIN entry, no re-auth. Anyone holding an unlocked handset has the full udhar book, including customer names and phone numbers, which are personal data under the DPDP Act 2023 for which this shop is the Data Fiduciary; §8(5) of that Act requires reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach. I state the obligation and not a verdict on sufficiency — whether a handset-level lock is the required safeguard is a legal reading I am not taking.

**Evidence.** packages/values/src/index.ts:58-69 (APP_LOCK_PIN_DIGITS = 4 with its rationale), and `grep -rn "APP_LOCK_PIN_DIGITS|appLock|PIN_" apps packages` returns that declaration and nothing else — no consumer; `grep -rniE "\b(pin|applock|biometric|screenLock)\b" apps packages` finds no lock screen; packages/schema/src/spec.ts:161-172 (users: one row, phone UNIQUE, shop_id) and :225-240 (entries have shop_id, day_id, section, amount, mode, party_id — no user_id or device_id); docs/spec/SPEC-DIGEST.md:179 and :352 (D9, one shop one phone, multi-device an explicit non-goal); apps/mobile/src/screens/registry.tsx lists eleven screens, none of them a lock.

**Recommendation.** Decide explicitly, in writing, whether V1 attributes entries to a person. If yes, the cheapest honest version is not accounts: it is an optional 'who is on the counter' selector on the shop record plus an actor column on entries, so the Behaviour Engine can at least segment. If no, then delete APP_LOCK_PIN_DIGITS or implement it — an unused security constant reads to the next reviewer as a shipped control — and write into the Behaviour Engine spec that every workflow metric is shop-level and must never be phrased to the owner as 'you'. Either way, ship the four-digit lock before the udhar book contains real customer phone numbers, and record in the DPDP notice that the handset is the access boundary.

### `HB-05` The retailer-defined business day is decided but unmodelled: days are still keyed by a calendar date, and shops has no start/end time

**HIGH** · gap

Decision 1 of 2026-10-22 makes the business day retailer-defined and names it a breaking change to dates.ts, D7 and the 77-vector corpus, with every existing day_id needing a migration. None of that has begun. The days table is still PRIMARY KEY (shop_id, date) with a DATE column, entries.day_id is a DATE, the shops table has no day_start_time or day_end_time, and dates.ts still carries ADR-004's reasoning in its header comment ('A shop’s day boundary is not a timezone question'). Only migration 0001 exists. The human cases this leaves broken are the ordinary ones, not the exotic ones: a sweet shop open till 01:30 during a festival, a dhaba whose day runs 11:00 to 02:00 every day of the year, a shop that extends its hours for a week in Kartik and reverts. Each of those puts real sales on the wrong side of a boundary and, as the decision itself says, breaks the reconciliation of two days at once — the shopkeeper sees a shortage one day and a surplus the next, and nothing in the app will tell him why.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:146-157 (CREATE TABLE shops: id, name, type, address, gstin, language, created_at, deletion_requested_at, deleted_at — no day boundary), :204-220 (days keyed by date), :225-240 (entries.day_id is a date); packages/formula-engine/src/dates.ts:1-12 (clock-free ISO-date arithmetic, header still cites ADR-004); packages/schema/migrations/ contains only 0001_init (up/down, postgres/sqlite) — no migration for the new day identity.

**Recommendation.** Model the business day as an opaque day_id with a recorded open timestamp and close timestamp, and keep the retailer's configured start time on the shop as a default rather than as the truth — a day belongs to the session that was open, not to a clock rule. Put the human cases in the migration's test set before writing the migration: a 00:40 sale on a 09:00-01:30 day; a shop that changes its closing time mid-week; a day left open 40 hours; two days whose configured windows overlap after a change. ### `HB-06` A shop that is shut has no way to say so, and the consistency scores will read a wedding or a monsoon as neglect

**HIGH** · risk

The document's own fairness rule is that no score may punish a user for being small. The same rule has a sibling the document does not state and the build does not implement: no score may punish a shop for being SHUT. A shop closed three days for a wedding, a seasonal shop that trades forty days around Diwali and then idles, a shop shut by a two-day bandh or a flooded street — all of these produce days with no entries and no closing. The day states are not_started / open / ready / closed / not_closed, and none of them means 'the shop did not trade'. Document §12D makes 'Active business days', 'Closing Consistency' and 'Recording Consistency' first-class measurements and §14 turns them into Operational Consistency, so unless a shut day is distinguishable the score reads a wedding as four days of non-compliance and tells the owner to be more consistent. That is the fastest way to make a shopkeeper stop trusting the scores, and once he distrusts one score he distrusts all three. Separately, T6 is specified to appear 'after a gap' but nothing anywhere defines what a gap is.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:39 (DAY_STATES = not_started, open, ready, closed, not_closed) and :214 (the CHECK that pins them); apps/mobile/src/screens/T6.tsx:1-14 (appears 'on the first run, and after a gap where there is no yesterday to carry from' — no definition of gap, and per HB-01 the screen has no production caller); the review document §12D and §14 as extracted at scratchpad/pdf/pdftotext.txt:309-317 and :367-380.

**Recommendation.** Add a 'shop closed' day state the owner can set, retrospectively and in bulk (a wedding is declared after the fact, from the train home). Exclude those days from every consistency denominator and say so on the score's explanation line. Define 'a gap' as a measured quantity on the shop, not a constant: the opening-cash question should return when there is no carried closing figure, which is a data condition, not a number of days. Add a seasonal-shop fixture to the corpus — forty active days then ninety idle — and assert that Operational Consistency on the reopening day is unchanged rather than floored.

### `HB-07` ADR-015's process-death banner is specified in detail and not built, so a dead battery mid-entry discards silently

**MEDIUM** · bug

## Security

*14 findings — 3 blocker, 8 high, 3 medium*

> I attacked the NEW surface the review document opens (typed adjustments, discrepancy records, refund settlement states, linked exchange components, the retailer-defined day boundary, Behaviour Engine telemetry) against the code that is actually built. Two blockers are pre-existing and they undermine every new table before it is written: (1) the API never sets `swafto.shop_id`, so the Row-Level Security layer the audit calls "the layer that survives an application bug" is inert in the running server — it is proven only against a probe role the test builds itself; (2) RLS is applied from a hardcoded table-name map and asserted by a hardcoded `relname IN (...)` list with `toBe(7)`, so any new table silently gets no policy and no failing test. On the new surface specifically: the discrepancy record can be suppressed entirely because "a difference requires a reason" lives only in the untrusted client while the server never re-derives expected cash at close, and `counted_cash` is COALESCE first-write-wins so a replayed `ready` day pins the figure the whole product exists to record; a typed adjustment cannot be an entry without weakening `entries_amount_ck` and `minimum: 0`, which the repo rules forbid; there is no date horizon anywhere, so any day in 0001..9999 can be created `open` and written into (unbounded backdating straight into the expected-cash equation); the sync batch is deliberately non-atomic and the client acks every row the server did not explicitly reject, so a half-exchange is the expected outcome and it inflates Sales Total exactly as §6 forbids; and there is no state-machine infrastructure at all — `days.state` is already client-supplied and unvalidated with a second derived source of truth, which is the pattern a four-state refund machine would inherit. 14 findings, all cited; 0 new tables exist today.

### `SEC-01` RLS is inert in the running API: `swafto.shop_id` is never set, so the whole second isolation layer is off

**BLOCKER** · security

Every tenancy policy is `USING (shop_id = current_setting('swafto.shop_id', true)::uuid)` (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:500-515). Nothing in the API ever sets that GUC — a grep for `set_config`/`SET LOCAL` across apps/api/src finds exactly one hit, `SET LOCAL swafto.erasure = 'on'` at apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:459. The server builds a plain shared `pg.Pool` and wraps it in a bare `{query}` with no per-request transaction (apps/api/src/server.ts:37-45). With the GUC unset, `current_setting(...,true)` is NULL, the predicate is NULL, and every policy denies every row — so the API can only be functional today because it connects as a superuser or owner role that is exempt. `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` covers the owner, so the live role is a superuser or BYPASSRLS. Either way layer 2 of the three-layer defence (SECURITY-AUDIT §2 TB5, "RLS is the layer that survives an application bug") is not in the request path. GUARD SCOPED BY: the connection's role and the presence of the GUC. A planted defect does NOT move along that axis — packages/schema/test/tenancy.spec.ts:127-163 creates its own `swafto_rls_probe` role, grants it, makes it the table owner and calls `set_config` by hand (lines 228, 240, 250), so it proves the policies are correct and proves nothing about how the application connects. This matters for the new surface because the entire protection story for adjustments, discrepancies, exchange groups and refund settlements will be "RLS covers it".

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/server.ts:37-45 (plain Pool, no transaction, no SET); apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:459 (the only SET LOCAL in the API, and it is `swafto.erasure`); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:500-515 (policies depend on `swafto.shop_id`); packages/schema/test/tenancy.spec.ts:127-163, 228 (the test builds and configures its own role); docs/audits/SECURITY-AUDIT.md §2 TB5 (claims RLS is the surviving layer)

**Recommendation.** Three changes, all small. (a) Connect the API as a dedicated `NOBYPASSRLS`, non-owner role; assert `rolsuper=false AND rolbypassrls=false` for the configured DATABASE_URL at boot and refuse to start otherwise. (b) Run every ledger request inside one transaction that begins with `SET LOCAL swafto.shop_id = <shopOf(req)>` — `SET LOCAL`, never `SET`, because a session-level GUC on a pooled connection leaks the previous request's tenant to the next one. (c) Add a case to tenancy.spec.ts that drives the real `pgRepo` over the real app's connection and asserts a cross-tenant read returns nothing; that is the plant axis currently unattacked. Do this before any new table, or every new table inherits a defence that is not running.

### `SEC-02` A new table gets no RLS policy and no failing test: the guard is scoped by a hardcoded table-name list in both the DDL and its test

**BLOCKER** · security

Tenancy DDL is generated by iterating a literal map of seven table names (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:499-515). The test that is supposed to prove it filters `pg_class` by the same seven names spelled out again and asserts `expect(res.rows.length).toBe(7)` (packages/schema/test/postgres.spec.ts:162-178). An eighth table — `adjustments`, `discrepancies`, `exchange_groups`, `refund_settlements`, `behaviour_events` — added to `tablesDDL` but not to `scopedBy` is created with no policy, no FORCE, and the RLS test still passes green with seven rows. GUARD SCOPED BY: the literal table name, in both the generator and the assertion. A planted defect (a new tenant-scoped table) does not move along that axis — this is structurally the same blindness docs/governance/REVIEW.md:44 records for `check-tooling` ("scoped by directory name — NO"). The only thing that forces a developer to notice is the unrelated `expect(names).toEqual([...])` table inventory at postgres.spec.ts:99-109, which they satisfy by adding one string to an array nine lines above the RLS list they will not think to edit. Note also that `shops` itself has no policy and no `shop_id` column, so the shop name, address and GSTIN of every tenant are readable by any role that can SELECT it — acceptable only while SEC-01 means RLS is off anyway, which is not a defence.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:499-515 (`scopedBy` literal map, seven entries); packages/schema/test/postgres.spec.ts:162-178 (`relname IN ('entries','days','parties','section_skips','devices','users','consents')`, `toBe(7)`); packages/schema/test/postgres.spec.ts:99-109 (the separate table inventory); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:146-157 (`shops` has no shop_id and is absent from `scopedBy`); docs/governance/REVIEW.md:44 (the identical name-scoped blindness, already recorded)

**Recommendation.** Invert the assertion so the axis becomes "every table" instead of "these names": query `pg_tables` for the schema, subtract an explicit `RLS_EXEMPT` array that carries a written reason per entry (`rate_events` — pre-authentication, keyed by HMAC, no shop; `shops` — decide and record), and fail if any remaining table lacks `relrowsecurity` and `relforcerowsecurity`. Then adding a table without a policy fails the build by construction. Add `shops` to `scopedBy` scoped through `id = current_setting('swafto.shop_id',...)`. Run `npm run prove:verifiers` after, and record the new axis in REVIEW.md's guard table.

### `SEC-03` The discrepancy record can be suppressed: "a difference requires a reason" exists only in the client, and the server never re-derives expected cash at close

**BLOCKER** · security

The review document §4 makes recording a discrepancy mandatory before a close with a non-zero difference. That rule is enforced in exactly one place: `Store.close()` on the handset (apps/mobile/src/store/index.ts:324-334), which compares `countedCash` against `expectedCash()` and throws without a reason. The server-side close path accepts `countedCash` and `differenceReason` straight from the body and writes them with no comparison at all (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:340-357). The only database guard is `days_closed_ck`, which requires `counted_cash IS NOT NULL AND closed_at IS NOT NULL` and nothing about the reason (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:217-219). So a crafted or modified client closes any day with any counted figure and no discrepancy record, and the response reports `daysWritten` so the handset acks and forgets. The API has the engine and uses it elsewhere (`computePrimary` at apps/api/src/read/routes.ts:111), so this is an omission, not a capability gap — and it contradicts the file's own stated posture: "THE SERVER RE-VALIDATES EVERYTHING, WITH THE SAME PARSERS THE PHONE USES" (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:18-23). The moment Cash Confidence is computed from discrepancy records (decision 4, review §14), suppression is score manipulation with no detection: a shop with zero recorded discrepancies is indistinguishable from a shop that never records one.

**Evidence.** apps/mobile/src/store/index.ts:324-334 (the only enforcement, on the untrusted side); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:340-357 (close path, no re-derivation); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:18-23 (the posture it violates); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:217-219 (`days_closed_ck` does not mention the reason); apps/api/src/read/routes.ts:111 (the server already runs `computePrimary`)

**Recommendation.** At the close step in sync/routes.ts, load the day's entries, run `expectedCashBalance` with the same engine, and refuse the close (per-day rejection, not a 500) when `countedCash !== expected` and no typed discrepancy accompanies it. Make the discrepancy its own append-only row referencing the day, carrying expected, actual, the typed reason and the server's own received timestamp — never a nullable column on `days`, because a nullable column is what makes absence and suppression look identical. Add the plant: close a day with a mismatched count and no reason over `app.inject()` and require a rejection; it passes today.

### `SEC-04` Counted cash and the discrepancy reason are COALESCE first-write-wins: a replayed `ready` day pins the day's truth permanently and silently

**HIGH** · bug

`upsertDay` merges with `counted_cash = COALESCE(EXCLUDED.counted_cash, days.counted_cash)` and the same for `difference_reason` and `closed_at` (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:249-254). The sync schema lets a client push a day in state `ready` carrying `countedCash` (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:121-124), and that path writes it directly (lines 230-239). So whichever push arrives first fixes `counted_cash` for ever; the real close that follows at lines 340-357 supplies its own `countedCash`, the COALESCE discards it, `days_closed_ck` is satisfied because the column is non-null, the route returns 200 and increments `daysWritten`, and the handset acks and drops the row. The shopkeeper's actual count is lost with no error on either side. The same applies to `difference_reason`: a second, different reason is silently ignored, so a correction to a wrong reason is impossible and invisible. This is a replay and ordering attack on precisely the record SEC-03 makes score-bearing, and it needs no attacker — two pushes in the wrong order do it.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:249-254 (COALESCE on counted_cash, difference_reason, closed_at); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:121-124, 230-239 (a `ready` day may carry countedCash and is written directly); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:343-353 (the close supplies countedCash that COALESCE will discard); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:217-219 (the CHECK is satisfied by any non-null value)

**Recommendation.** Two separable fixes. (a) Make `counted_cash` writable only by the close transition — reject `countedCash` on any day whose state is not `closed` at the schema, so `ready` can never carry it. (b) Replace silent COALESCE-wins with an explicit refusal: if an incoming `countedCash` differs from a stored non-null one, reject that day with a named reason rather than ignoring it, the same way `appendEntry` already refuses an id resent with different content (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:329-340). Precedent exists in the file; the day path just does not follow it.

### `SEC-05` A typed adjustment cannot be an `entries` row without weakening `entries_amount_ck` and `minimum: 0` — and weakening a check is forbidden

**HIGH** · requirement

An adjustment moves cash in either direction. Every amount in this system is non-negative by invariant — "INTEGER PAISE. Always non-negative - sign is carried by the section" (packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:56-57) — enforced at three layers: the sync schema `amount: { type: 'integer', minimum: 0 }` (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:86), the DB `entries_amount_ck CHECK (wholePaise(amount))` (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:241), and the parsers. The obvious implementation of a signed adjustment relaxes all three, and CLAUDE.md's first Never forbids exactly that. A signed `amount` column would also silently un-bound every other section at the same time, because the CHECK is per-column, not per-section — the axis REVIEW.md:37 records for the append-only trigger ("scoped by column").

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:56-57 (the invariant, stated); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:86 (`minimum: 0`); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:241 (`entries_amount_ck`); packages/formula-engine/src/primary.ts:121-153 (`expectedCashBalance` carries sign per line in its `sign` field, never in the amount); CLAUDE.md "Never weaken a test or a check to make it pass"

**Recommendation.** Keep `amount` non-negative and carry the sign in the section, as the engine already does. Add two sections, `adjustment_in` and `adjustment_out`, and a typed-reason column constrained by a CHECK that pins each adjustment type to exactly one direction (a `short_count` type can never be an `adjustment_in`) — so a type cannot be used to push money the way its name does not mean. Add both as their own `working.adjustment_in` / `working.adjustment_out` lines inside `expectedCashBalance`, because a line that does not appear in the `Figure` working is a line that absorbs a difference invisibly, which is the laundering path decision 2 exists to close. Extend `SECTION_MODES` so an adjustment is cash-only or UPI-only and never `udhar`/`shop_stock`.

### `SEC-06` No date horizon anywhere: any day in 0001..9999 can be created `open` and written into, which is unbounded backdating into the expected-cash equation

**HIGH** · security

The server validates that a day_id is a syntactically real date and nothing more. `date` is length-checked at 10 chars in the sync schema (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:118), `parseIsoDate(day.date)` checks only calendar validity (line 201), and `entrySchema.dayId` is the same length check (line 80). There is no comparison against `deps.now()`, which the app has injected for exactly this kind of decision (apps/api/src/app.ts:39-40). So a client creates `{date: '2019-04-01', state: 'open'}`, the route upserts it (lines 230-239), and then posts entries into it — the closed-day trigger does not fire because the day was never closed, and the FK is satisfied because the client just created the day. The same works forward: `2099-12-31`. Today the damage is a distorted history. With typed adjustments (decision 2) it becomes money moved into a period already reported, and with the Behaviour Engine's "Active business days" and "Closing Consistency" (review §12D) it becomes direct score manipulation — a far-future open day also sits permanently un-closeable, dragging Operational Consistency. This is also the hole the retailer-defined boundary migration has to close rather than widen.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:118 (`date` minLength/maxLength 10 only), 201 (`parseIsoDate` validity only), 80 (`dayId` same), 230-239 (the day is written on the client's word); apps/api/src/app.ts:39-40 (`now` is already injected and unused for this); packages/formula-engine/src/dates.ts:18 (`parseIsoDate` is a calendar check, not a window check)

**Recommendation.** Add one server-side window, derived from `deps.now()` so the formula engine stays clock-free (ADR-012): refuse any `day_id` in the future, and refuse one more than N days behind (N is a product decision, not mine to invent — see SEC-14). Separately refuse creating a *new* day older than the shop's newest closed day, since a day that predates a completed reconciliation can only be a correction, and the product already has one shape for that: a correction entry dated today with `corrects_day_id` pointing back (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:235, and the trigger comment at spec.ts:416-424 that deliberately allows exactly this). The plant: push a 2019 day and a 2099 day and require both rejected.

### `SEC-07` Exchange: the sync batch is deliberately non-atomic and the client acks every row not explicitly rejected, so a committed half-exchange is the expected outcome

**HIGH** · bug

Review §6 requires one business event with linked components so Sales Total cannot inflate. The sync route is explicitly designed not to be atomic — "A batch is not a transaction: one malformed entry must not send back a day's worth of good ones" (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:324-331) — and loops per entry with per-entry rejection (lines 262-321). The client then computes `accepted = rows.filter((r) => !refused.has(payloadId(r)))` (apps/mobile/src/net/client.ts:357-358) and the host acks exactly those (apps/mobile/src/host.tsx:195), so the accepted leg is dropped from the outbox while the refused leg stays queued. If the return leg of an exchange trips any per-entry refusal — `entries_party_ck` (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:248-252), a mode/section disagreement (routes.ts:295-307), the closed-day trigger — the server is left holding the sale leg alone, permanently, and its Sales Total is inflated by exactly the amount §6 says must not inflate it. Nothing detects this: the device shows the complete exchange, the server shows half, and SECURITY-AUDIT T2.3's "server-side divergence alarm" would read a legitimate split as client tampering, which is worse than silence.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:324-331 (not a transaction, by design and for a good reason); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:262-321 (per-entry try/catch, per-entry rejection); apps/mobile/src/net/client.ts:357-358 (accepted = everything not refused); apps/mobile/src/host.tsx:191-198 (acks the accepted set, keeps the refused); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:248-252 (a party check that can refuse one leg and not the other); docs/audits/SECURITY-AUDIT.md §2 TB2 T2.3 (the divergence alarm this would confuse)

**Recommendation.** Keep per-entry rejection for independent entries and make the unit of atomicity the group, not the batch. Give each exchange component a `group_id` and a `group_size`, validate the whole group in the route before writing any of it, and reject every member if any member fails — so the client acks none of them and retries the whole group, which terminates because the refusal is deterministic. Enforce it in the database too, with a trigger or a deferred constraint that refuses a committed group whose member count does not equal `group_size`, and make the engine refuse to compute from an incomplete group rather than silently summing the half it can see. Test case: push a two-leg exchange with one leg deliberately invalid and assert the server holds zero of it and the outbox still holds both.

### `SEC-08` There is no state-machine infrastructure at all, and `days.state` already demonstrates the failure mode a four-state refund machine would inherit

**HIGH** · gap

Review §7 asks for four settlement states. The codebase has never validated a state transition. `days.state` is accepted as whatever arrives in the body (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:230-239, schema enum at 121) with no transition check anywhere — a grep for the state literals finds only the enum declaration, the port type, a read-route default and the engine's own derivation (packages/formula-engine/src/index.ts:119-120). So `ready` -> `not_started` -> `ready` is accepted, and `ready` is accepted on a day with no opening cash even though the engine would derive `not_started` for it: there are already two sources of truth for day state, one stored and client-supplied, one derived, and the D7 freeze reads the stored one. A settlement state stored as a client-sent field on the same pattern gets, for free: illegal transitions (`Refund paid` -> `Pending settlement`), refund of a refund, refund exceeding the original, and double refund via two components with different ids claiming the same original. The only existing guard with the right shape is `entries_no_insert_into_closed_day`, which REVIEW.md:38 records as scoped by "day state, and the day named by the row" — nothing is scoped by an entity's own transition history.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:121, 230-239 (state written on the client's word); packages/formula-engine/src/index.ts:119-120 (a second, derived source of truth for day state); grep across apps/api/src and packages/formula-engine for the state literals returns only declarations and defaults — no transition validation exists; docs/governance/REVIEW.md:37-38 (the guard axes that do exist)

**Recommendation.** Do not store the settlement state. Derive it from append-only settlement events referencing the original sale, exactly as every other figure in this product is derived on read — then an illegal transition is unrepresentable rather than rejected, and `no stored totals` (CLAUDE.md) applies unchanged. Enforce the money bounds in the database, where a replay cannot get around them: a trigger that refuses a settlement when the sum of settlements against that original exceeds the original's amount, and a CHECK that a settlement's target is a sale and never another settlement (blocking refund-of-refund structurally). Double refund via replay is then already handled by the client-minted id and `ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING` (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:300-321). Add the illegal-transition and over-refund plants; neither has anywhere to fail today.

### `SEC-09` `upsertParty` has no shop guard on its ON CONFLICT branch — the audit claims one that does not exist — and it can deadlock a shop's sync permanently

**HIGH** · security

SECURITY-AUDIT §2 TB5 row T5.4 states "the upsert predicate is `(id)` and the row is verified to belong to the session's `shop_id` before any write is accepted". It is not. `upsertParty` is `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (id) DO UPDATE SET name = EXCLUDED.name, phone = COALESCE(...)` with no `WHERE parties.shop_id = ...` anywhere (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:382-390), and `parties.id` is a global primary key rather than `(shop_id, id)` (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:189-199). So the only thing standing between shop B and overwriting shop A's customer name is RLS — which per SEC-01 is not running. Two consequences. (a) With RLS off: a cross-tenant write, S-11 by name, through a route the audit records as covered. (b) With RLS on: Postgres evaluates the conflict against the real index and then fails the policy check, `translate()` does not map an RLS error code (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:88-103, which handles 23505/23514/23503/P0001 and rethrows everything else), and the party loop in the route has no try/catch at all (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:250-259) — so the throw escapes the handler, the whole push 500s, the client never acks, and the outbox retries the identical batch for ever. One colliding or crafted party id permanently stops a shopkeeper's ledger reaching the server. Every new table the document adds (exchange groups, settlements, adjustments) will be upserted the same way unless the pattern is fixed first.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:382-390 (no shop_id predicate on the DO UPDATE); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:189-199 (`id uuid PRIMARY KEY`, shop_id is a separate column); docs/audits/SECURITY-AUDIT.md §2 TB5 T5.4 (claims the check that is absent); apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:88-103 (`translate` rethrows an unmapped code); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:250-259 (the party loop has no try/catch, unlike the entry loop at 262-321)

**Recommendation.** Add `WHERE parties.shop_id = EXCLUDED.shop_id` to the ON CONFLICT clause — one line, and it makes the repository layer actually scope the write instead of delegating to RLS. Wrap the party loop in the same per-row rejection the entry loop uses so a bad row is a `rejected` entry and not a 500. Map 42501 in `translate()` to a `WriteRefused` so an RLS refusal can never become an unhandled throw. Correct the T5.4 row in SECURITY-AUDIT.md: it currently records a control that was never built, which is the "defence asserted and never attacked" shape apps/api/src/app.ts:61-65 already names.

### `SEC-11` Retailer-defined day boundary: if `day_id` is derived at read time the boundary becomes a money-moving control that bypasses D7 without touching a frozen row

**HIGH** · risk

Decision 1 makes the business day retailer-defined. `day_id` is today a client-assigned calendar date and a primary-key component (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:204-219, `PRIMARY KEY (shop_id, date)`; the invariant is stated at packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:36). Every freeze guard is scoped by the day the row names: `entries_no_insert_into_closed_day` reads `NEW.day_id` (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:466-479), and REVIEW.md:38 records that axis as "day state, and the day named by the row". If the migration makes `day_id` a function of a mutable boundary plus a timestamp, then moving the boundary re-buckets already-recorded entries: a 00:30 transaction can be moved out of a closed day or into one, the closed day's figures change, and no UPDATE ever touches the frozen `days` row — so `days_closed_immutable` (spec.ts:454-463) never fires and D7 is bypassed without being violated. That also reverses the reconciliation of two days at once, which is the exact harm decision 1 was taken to prevent. Combined with SEC-06 (no date horizon) a boundary change plus a backdated day is a general-purpose tool for moving money between periods.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:204-219 (day is the PK component), 466-479 (the freeze trigger keys on `NEW.day_id`), 454-463 (`days_closed_immutable` only fires on an UPDATE of the days row); packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:36 ("Client-assigned; never rewritten"); docs/governance/REVIEW.md:38 (the guard's scoping axis); CLAUDE.md decision 1

**Recommendation.** Stamp, never derive. The boundary is shop configuration stored append-only with an `effective_from` date, and `day_id` is computed once at write time from the boundary in force then and is immutable thereafter — which keeps the existing PK, the existing FK and both freeze triggers correct with no change. Refuse any boundary change whose `effective_from` is on or before the shop's newest closed day, and refuse a retroactive one outright. Add the plant REVIEW.md's convention asks for: close a day, change the boundary, and assert that no closed day's figures and no entry's `day_id` moved. There is no such test today because there is no boundary today.

### `SEC-13` Workflow Behaviour telemetry is a new purpose outside the itemised notice, and every score input is a client-asserted value

**HIGH** · law

Review §12C asks the Behaviour Engine to measure workflows started, completed, abandoned, time to completion, validation failures, corrections and repeated attempts. Measuring abandonment means recording that something was started and never finished — which is the draft state §3 says must never become a record, now leaving the device as telemetry instead. Consent today has exactly three purposes: `account_and_ledger`, `sms_reminders`, `crash_diagnostics` (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:89-93). Behavioural telemetry is none of them. The DPDP Act 2023 requires an itemised notice of the personal data and the purpose (s.5) and consent that is "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous ... for the specified purpose" (s.6(1)); shipping abandonment events under `account_and_ledger` is purpose creep and the conservative reading is that it needs its own notice item and its own withdrawal. Separately, the score inputs are all client-asserted: `closedAt` is accepted from the body with only a length check (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:126), as are `state` and entry `createdAt` (lines 93, 121). "Closing Consistency" and "Active business days" (review §12D) computed from those are computed from numbers the measured party chooses.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:89-93 (three consent purposes, none behavioural); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:126 (`closedAt` body-supplied, length check only), 121 (`state` likewise), 93 (`createdAt` likewise); docs/audits/SECURITY-AUDIT.md §5 (the logging allow-list bans amounts, so these events cannot ride the log path); DPDP Act 2023 s.5 (notice) and s.6(1) (specific consent) — cited as the sections; I have not re-read the 2025 Rules for a notice-granularity rule and take the conservative reading

**Recommendation.** Compute Workflow Behaviour entirely on the device from local-only events and sync nothing but the derived score — the outbox/rate_events split already establishes the device-local-only pattern (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:306-318: "Exists on the device and must NOT exist on the server"). If any raw event must leave the device, add a fourth consent purpose with its own itemised notice line and its own withdrawal, and never put an amount in an event. For the scores, record both the client's `closed_at` (what the shopkeeper saw, which is correct for display — the reasoning at apps/mobile/src/store/index.ts:340-345 holds) and a server `received_at`, and compute every consistency metric from the server stamp.

### `SEC-10` `appendEntry`'s duplicate read-back is unscoped by shop, so the repository layer is not scoping it either

**MEDIUM** · security

When an insert hits `ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING`, the code reads the stored row back to decide whether this is a harmless replay or an attempt to edit an entry: `SELECT * FROM entries WHERE id = $1` with no `shop_id` predicate (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:327). `shopId` is in scope on the line above. Per SEC-01 RLS is not active, so with a colliding id the comparison runs against another shop's row and produces one of two wrong answers: a `WriteRefused` whose message is derived from another tenant's record, or `{inserted: false}` — reported to the client as `entriesAlreadyPresent` (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:311, 362), acked, and dropped from the outbox, silently swallowing a real sale. UUIDv7 makes accidental collision negligible, so the severity today is the architectural one: SECURITY-AUDIT §2 TB5 names three isolation layers and says of the first that "every query goes through a repository that takes `shopId` as a required first argument; there is no way to call it without one" — true of the signature, false of the query. The repository/RLS test split means no plant moves along this axis: tenancy.spec.ts attacks raw SQL, repo-equivalence attacks behaviour, and neither drives the repository as a non-superuser tenant.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:327 (`WHERE id = $1`, no shop scope, with shopId in scope); apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:300-321 (the DO NOTHING that reaches it); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:309-311, 362 (a non-insert is reported as already-present and acked); docs/audits/SECURITY-AUDIT.md §2 TB5 ("the three isolation layers", layer 1)

**Recommendation.** Add `AND shop_id = $2`. Then sweep every `SELECT`/`ON CONFLICT` in apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts for the same shape and make the shop predicate mandatory in the repository rather than inherited from RLS — defence in depth means each layer holds alone. Add one tenancy case that drives `pgRepo` (not raw SQL) as a NOBYPASSRLS tenant; it is the plant axis SEC-01 leaves open and it will cover every new table's repository for free.

### `SEC-12` Free-text `difference_reason` is an uncontrolled personal-data channel that reaches the DPDP export; typing adjustments without typing this leaves the laundering channel open

**MEDIUM** · security

Decision 2 types adjustments so no free-form value can absorb a discrepancy. The discrepancy reason itself is still free text: `difference_reason text` on the days table (packages/schema/src/spec.ts:210) accepted as any string up to 200 characters (apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:125). (a) It defeats the purpose it was typed for: if the *reason* is free text, the audit trail for a non-zero difference is unstructured and a Cash Confidence score cannot distinguish an honest short count from a note, so the typed adjustment list closes one laundering channel and leaves the one beside it open. (b) It is a personal-data channel nobody designed: a shopkeeper will write a customer's name or phone there, it is never redacted (the logger allow-list at apps/api/src/app.ts:126-137 covers `phone`, `code` and auth headers only), and `exportUser` returns whole `days` rows (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:400-409) so it flows into the DPDP access response under a purpose the itemised notice does not describe.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:210 (`difference_reason text`); apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:125 (any string, 200 chars); apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:400-409 (`exportUser` selects whole days rows); apps/api/src/app.ts:126-137 (the redaction allow-list does not include it); apps/mobile/src/store/index.ts:317-322 (the code already flags the reason list as an open product decision, citing PRODUCT-REVIEW recommendation 1)

**Recommendation.** Type the discrepancy reason from the same fixed list as the adjustments, and include an explicit "I am not sure" member — store/index.ts:317-322 already argues for it, and forcing a false reason writes a wrong fact into a ledger that cannot be edited. Keep free text out of the day record entirely; if a note is wanted later it belongs in a separate, clearly-noticed, erasable field, not in the reconciliation record. Typed values also make the Cash Confidence input countable, which decision 4 requires before production use.

### `SEC-14` Unanswered before an adjustment can be built: its bound, and the backdating window

**MEDIUM** · decision-needed

Two numbers decide whether a typed adjustment is a control or a hole, and neither the review document nor the repo states them, so neither may be invented. (a) The magnitude bound. D9 makes this one shop, one phone, one user (docs/audits/SECURITY-AUDIT.md §8), so there is no second role to authorise anything — "who may create an adjustment" has only one answer, the shopkeeper. The constraint therefore cannot be authorisation; it has to be a bound plus visibility. An unbounded adjustment sets Cash Difference to zero for any count, which makes expected-vs-actual and Cash Confidence meaningless — the exact harm CLAUDE.md decision 2 was taken to prevent, arriving through the amount instead of through the reason. A bound as an absolute figure punishes the small retailer that review §14 says must not be punished, so it probably has to be relative (a share of the day's cash movement, or of opening cash) with a per-day count cap. (b) The backdating window N from SEC-06. Both are product decisions with no measured basis in this repo, and CLAUDE.md forbids printing a number I did not measure.

**Evidence.** docs/audits/SECURITY-AUDIT.md §8 ("D9 makes one shop, one phone an explicit non-goal" for multi-device — there is no second actor); CLAUDE.md decision 2 (the reason for typing adjustments at all); the review document §14 (no score may punish a user for being small); no bound or window exists anywhere in packages/schema/src/spec.ts or apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts today

**Recommendation.** Ask the document's author for both, in the same message as the §4 UPI-in-cash-received defect already flagged in CLAUDE.md. Propose as the default to be confirmed: an adjustment is capped as a share of the day's recorded cash movement rather than as an absolute rupee figure (so a 5,000/day shop and a 50,000/day shop are bounded proportionally), at most one adjustment per type per day, and any day containing an adjustment is surfaced in the Level-3 "where is pressure appearing" output rather than being silently reconciled. Until both numbers are decided, do not ship the adjustment write path — an unbounded one is strictly worse than no adjustment at all, because it reconciles.

### `BE-05` Cash Confidence, defined: three published ratios, no weight required

**HIGH** · requirement

Window W = the trailing 30 closed days, or all closed days when fewer than 30. Gate at 14 closed days, reusing gate(). ### `BE-07` Business Health: refuse a scalar in V1 and ship the five already-built components as a labelled panel

**HIGH** · decision-needed

Review doc 14 calls Business Health a 'broad composite indicator' and says in the same breath that the scores must not go to production until their mathematics is validated. A composite is exactly the part that cannot be validated, because it cannot be computed from recorded facts alone. Turning BI1's direction, BI2's coefficient of variation, BI3's deficit-day count, BI4's pressure ratio and BI6's top-three share into one number requires three choices that no fact in the ledger can settle: a weight per component, a mapping from each ratio onto a common scale, and a cut-off that separates healthy from not. **Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/intelligence.ts:64 profitTrend, :84 salesConsistency, :101 cashFlowStability, :129 workingCapitalPressure, :220 creditConcentrationTrend — all five implemented and returning Gated<T>. docs/handover/DEFERRED.md:73 records the BI engine as implemented, gated and vector-covered at 87% statement coverage. ### `BE-08` Size fairness: every score metric must be a ratio over the same shop's own window, and a check should enforce it

**HIGH** · requirement

The naive version fails in two distinct ways and both punish the small shop. First, absolute bands: any rule of the form 'daily sales above X scores well' puts the 5,000-a-day retailer below every band the 50,000-a-day retailer clears, for running a smaller shop competently. Second, and less obvious, absolute tolerances: a 50-rupee miscount is 1% of the small shop's day and 0.1% of the large one's, so a flat 'within 100 rupees' tolerance scores identical care ten times worse for the smaller shop, and a flat percentage scores a 5-rupee rounding slip as a 0.1% failure for the shop whose whole day is 5,000. Every metric in BE-05 and BE-06 is therefore a ratio over the same shop's own window, with no constant rupee figure anywhere in it. CC-B divides a median gap by a median day's sales; OC-B divides against 8 times the window length, which scales with the shop's own activity and nobody else's.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/money.ts:102-110 — ratio() keeps numeratorPaise and denominatorPaise alongside basisPoints, so every score can render its own working and a reviewer can recompute it by hand. BI2 and BI4 already follow this shape (intelligence.ts:84-98, 129-137). CLAUDE.md:43-49 — all money arithmetic goes through the audited helpers and check:money fails on a bare operator, in source and in tests.

**Recommendation.** Add one verifier, a sibling of check:money: fail the build on a numeric paise literal compared against a derived money value inside packages/formula-engine/src/scores.ts. One planted-defect case for prove:verifiers. That turns 'no absolute benchmark' from a principle in a document into something the build refuses to break.

### `BE-10` Derive-on-read is affordable for one day and not for a thirty-day score, because one day's input already replays all of history

**HIGH** · risk

'No stored totals, derive on read' is right and should not be weakened. But the current read path builds a single day's input by fetching every entry the shop has ever recorded and then replaying every past day in a loop to carry credit and supplier outstanding forward. That is O(N) in all history for one day. A score over 30 closed days, built by the obvious route of calling that function thirty times, is O(30N) and replays the same history thirty times over. On the target 7,000-rupee handset, and against a Postgres query with no LIMIT and no projection, that is the shape that turns a 250 ms day-close into a visible stall. Nothing here is a reason to cache a total; it is a reason to do the one replay once.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/read/routes.ts:56-73 — dayInputFor calls repo.entriesBefore(shopId, date) and then loops over every distinct earlier day recomputing creditOutstanding and supplierOutstanding. apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:353-359 — entriesBefore is 'SELECT * FROM entries WHERE shop_id = $1 AND day_id < $2 ORDER BY day_id, created_at, id', with no limit and no projection. The thresholds this threatens are B16 (Today screen, 365 days history, <= 400 ms), B19 (full nine-section day-close compute, <= 250 ms), B20 (reports render, 365 closed days, <= 1200 ms), B21 (aggregate query over 10,000 entries, <= 100 ms) and B22 (peak memory during day close, <= 220 MB) in docs/benchmarks/BENCHMARKS.md. Every one of those five is recorded as blocked and unmeasured: docs/benchmarks/BENCHMARKS-RESULTS.md:398-404.

**Recommendation.** One pass, not thirty: fetch entriesBefore(end) once, fold forward, and emit the whole ClosedDaySummary[] from that single replay; the scores then read an in-memory array and touch no store. Add a projection and a date floor to the query so a shop with three years of history does not pull all of it for a 30-day window. Propose no change to any threshold. Because B16 and B19-B22 have never been measured, there is no measured headroom to spend — instrument B21 against a 10,000-entry device database before the scores ship, so the first reading is taken against the code that will carry them.

### `W2` ackOutbox keys the day row on a stable id, so a close recorded mid-push is silently dropped

**BLOCKER** · bug

The day outbox row id is `'ob-day-' + day.date` (store/index.ts:177) - stable across every content change to that day. `memoryPort.putDay` replaces any pending row with that id (line 106), and `ackOutbox` removes rows by id (lines 110-113). `Host.sync()` reads the queue, awaits the push, then acks (host.tsx:184-195). So if the shopkeeper closes the day while the push is in flight - seconds on 2G, and closing is exactly what he does at the end of a day he has been syncing - `putDay` replaces the queued OPEN record with the CLOSED one under the same id, and the ack for the open row that was actually sent deletes the closed row that was not. The close exists on the phone, the server never hears about it, and nothing reports a problem. Entries are immune because each gets a unique row id; only the day row has this shape, and the day row carries opening cash and the close - the two figures the product exists to record. The file's own comment at store/index.ts:71-76 says a day reaching the server late was already a real defect once.

**Evidence.** apps/mobile/src/store/index.ts:176-178, 99-107, 110-113 - apps/mobile/src/host.tsx:184-195. Confirmed by probe: queued ids were captured, close() was called, then ackOutbox(capturedIds) left pendingOutbox.length === 0 with day.state === 'closed'. The assertion passed.

**Recommendation.** Ack by content, not by id. Either make the day row id include a revision (`ob-day-<date>-<rev>`), so a replacement cannot be acked by its predecessor's id, or have `ackOutbox` ignore any row whose `queuedAt` is newer than the drain that is acking it. The second is the smaller diff: `Host.sync()` already has the queue snapshot it sent.

### `EX-2` P4 Net Sales is a sum of three '+' terms with no minus term anywhere — this is the exact mechanism by which a naive two-sale exchange inflates Sales Total, and it also manufactures a phantom cash shortfall

**BLOCKER** · bug

`netSales` (primary.ts:156-163) is three working lines, all `sign: '+'`: cash_sales + upi_sales + udhar_sales. `figure()` (primary.ts:41-48) will happily subtract, but nothing in P4 ever asks it to. There is no contra-revenue term in the engine at all.

Worked case — a customer swaps a ₹900 shirt bought earlier for a ₹1,200 shirt and pays ₹300 cash. What a developer with only the current vocabulary can record is a ₹1,200 sale, because that is the only row that represents goods leaving the shop. Two independent failures follow from that one row:

(1) **Revenue double-count.** The ₹900 shirt was already counted in P4 on the day it was first sold. Recording the ₹1,200 replacement as a sale makes P4 report ₹2,100 across the two days for ₹1,200 of goods that actually left the shop. Net Sales is inflated by ₹900 — the review's §6 warning, exactly. It propagates: `estimatedProfit` (primary.ts:263-269) is P4 − P5 − P6, so profit is overstated by ₹900 too; `businessMovement` (operational.ts:238-253) compares an inflated today against an inflated median; `toClosedDaySummary` (index.ts:142-162) freezes the inflated figure into the BI window.

(2) **Phantom cash shortfall.** If that ₹1,200 row is `cash_sales`, `expectedCashBalance` (primary.ts:126) adds ₹1,200 to the expected drawer. Only ₹300 was handed over. At closing, `dailyCashHealth` (operational.ts:95-115) reports the till ₹900 short and sets `reasonRequired: true` — the only blocking step in the system — for a day in which nothing was lost. The shopkeeper is asked to explain a discrepancy the app invented. That is the precise failure mode the file's own header warns about at primary.ts:9-11 and it will recur every exchange, forever.

The root cause is that one row is being asked to carry two different facts: goods out, and money in. For an ordinary sale those are the same number, which is why nobody has had to separate them yet. For an exchange they are not.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/primary.ts:156-163 (P4, three '+' lines, no '-'); primary.ts:41-48 (figure() supports '-', never used in P4); primary.ts:126 (cash_sales feeds expected cash); operational.ts:95-115 (dailyCashHealth, reasonRequired on non-zero); primary.ts:263-269 (profit = P4-P5-P6)

**Recommendation.** Separate goods from money at the schema level, and give P4 its first minus term. Four new sections, zero new modes:

- `sales_return` — goods came back, at the price they were sold for. Mode forced to `other` so D2 keeps it out of the drawer. Enters P4 with **sign '-'**.
- `exchange_out` — replacement goods went out, at the replacement price. Mode forced to `other`. Enters P4 with sign '+'.
- `exchange_receipt` — the customer paid the shop the net difference. Modes cash/upi/other. Enters P1/P3 when mode=cash. **Never** touches P4.
- `refund_paid` — the shop paid the customer out, issued credit, or settled in goods. Modes cash/upi/udhar/other. Enters P2/P3 when mode=cash. **Never** touches P4, and **never** touches P6 or profit.

The ₹900/₹1,200/₹300 case then becomes three rows under one event: sales_return 900, exchange_out 1200, exchange_receipt 300 cash. P4 moves by +300 (the real revenue change), expected cash moves by +300 (the real money), profit moves by +300. Nothing is inflated and the till reconciles.

`refund_paid` staying out of P6 is the D3 rule applied to a second event, and a developer will "correct" it for the same reason they correct D3. Give it a named test in the style of `d3-withdrawal-excluded-from-profit` — `refund-excluded-from-expenses` — and a comment block at the P6 site mirroring primary.ts:184.

### `EX-3` There is no entry-to-entry link on either engine; `corrects_day_id` is a DATE, so 'one business event with linked components' has nothing to link with

**BLOCKER** · gap

The `entries` table has exactly one linkage column, `corrects_day_id DATE` (spec.ts:235, emitted at 0001_init.postgres.up.sql:79), with an index on it (spec.ts:364). **Recommendation.** Add one column, not a join table: `event_id UUID NULL` on `entries`, plus an index `(shop_id, event_id)`. NULL for every one of the eight existing sections — nothing about them changes and the 77 vectors are untouched. NOT NULL and shared by all legs for the four new sections, enforced by CHECK: `event_id IS NOT NULL OR section NOT IN ('sales_return','exchange_out','exchange_receipt','refund_paid')`. It would be a second source of truth about an event's existence and would need its own freeze and tenancy rules; a shared id on the rows that already exist needs neither. Skip a row-level `corrects_entry_id` for now unless another dimension needs it — add when a shopkeeper has to correct one line of a four-line day.

### `EX-5` The sync endpoint appends entries one at a time and documents that it is deliberately not a transaction — a partial exchange can therefore land on the server and inflate Sales Total permanently

**BLOCKER** · bug

`apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:262` opens `for (const raw of body.entries ?? [])`, each iteration parses and calls `deps.repo.appendEntry(shopId, entry)` independently at :309, catching per-entry failures into a `rejected` array. The comment at :324 states the design: "200, NOT 207 OR 400, EVEN WITH REJECTIONS. A batch is not a transaction: one malformed entry must not send back a day's worth of good ones." `appendEntry` (apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:291-322) is a single `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING` with no surrounding transaction.

That reasoning is correct for a day's batch of independent events and wrong for one event's legs. If an exchange's three legs arrive in one batch and the `exchange_out` leg is accepted while the `sales_return` leg is rejected — a bad party id, a `WriteRefused` from a CHECK, an amount that fails `assertPaise` — the server now holds goods-out with no goods-in. P4 on the server is inflated by the replacement price, with no error visible to the shopkeeper, who got a 200. The client keeps the rejected id and stops retrying it (:325-327), so the half-event is permanent.

The client's own queue cannot help: `outbox` is keyed `entry_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY` (spec.ts:313), one row per entry, with no notion of a group, so it cannot hold three legs or release them together.

This matters even though the phone is authoritative (SPEC-DIGEST:348 "The local write is the write"), because the server copy is what a restore reads. A restore of a partial exchange is a permanently wrong Sales Total that no entry on the phone explains.

**Evidence.** apps/api/src/sync/routes.ts:261-262 (per-entry loop), :309 (appendEntry per entry), :324-327 ("A batch is not a transaction"); apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:291-322 (appendEntry, no transaction); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:313 (outbox, one row per entry); docs/spec/SPEC-DIGEST.md:348-351

**Recommendation.** Keep the existing property and add the narrow one. Group `body.entries` by `event_id` before the loop; append each group inside one transaction, all-or-nothing, and reject the whole group on any leg's failure with one `rejected` row per leg so the phone knows. Entries with a NULL `event_id` — every one of the eight existing sections — take exactly the path they take today, so the comment at :324 stays true as written for them and gets one sentence added for events. Shortest diff that fixes it; nothing else in the handler moves.

On the client, make the outbox release an event's legs together — either an `event_id` column on `outbox` or by draining in event groups. One runnable check: push a two-leg exchange whose second leg violates a CHECK and assert the server holds zero of the two.

So this is not a half-built feature with a design to recover — it is a new domain concept introduced by the review document, and it is the first concept in SWAFTO that requires one business event to span several ledger rows. Everything built so far has the property that one entry = one business event, and four separate mechanisms quietly depend on it (see EX-3, EX-5, EX-6, EX-7).

The §15 spec rows the review asks for, for these two workflows:

| Workflow | Objective | Measurements | Outputs | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchange | Record one swap of goods with its net money difference, without inflating revenue | returned value, replacement value, net difference, direction (receipt/payable), settlement completeness | Exchange movement; Exchange Receivable / Payable outstanding | Review open exchange differences |
| Return / Refund | Reverse a sale when goods come back, and record how (or whether) it was settled | returned value, settlement mode, settlement completeness, days pending, return share of sales | Return rate; Refund Payable outstanding; credit notes issued | Review unsettled returns |

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/ (8 files, none matching); apps/mobile/src/screens/ (11 screens, none matching); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:43-54 (the eight sections, no return/exchange); docs/spec/source/SWAFTO-System-Architecture-and-DFD.txt:192,344 ("exchange" = data exchange only)

**Recommendation.** Treat this as new-domain work, not a gap-fill. Before any code: write an ADR that states the one-entry-one-event assumption explicitly and records that Exchange breaks it, because four existing mechanisms rest on it and none of them documents that it does. Then design per EX-2 through EX-9. Do not start with a screen.

### `EX-4` The four settlement states must be a derived fold over the event's legs, never a stored status — and then D1 append-only costs nothing, because there is no state to UPDATE

**HIGH** · requirement

Checked and true: there is no state machine anywhere in the build. The only lifecycle is the day's, and `derivedDayState` (index.ts:116-121) *derives* it — `if (stored === 'closed' || stored === 'not_closed') return stored; if (openingCash === null) return 'not_started'; return cashCheckUnlocked(input) ? 'ready' : 'open'`. Its own comment at index.ts:111-115 gives the reason: deriving rather than latching means a deletion that returns a section to untouched correctly walks the day back from ready to open. `sectionStates` (index.ts:49-61) does the same thing for the three per-section states. The pattern exists twice already.

So the question "what does a state machine cost given D1 append-only" has a zero answer, provided the state is never stored. A `settlement_status` column would be unwritable: `entries_no_update_or_delete` (0001_init.postgres.up.sql:158-160) raises on any UPDATE, and Postgres additionally has `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON entries` (:240). Pending → Refunded would be a forbidden write. Derived, it is a fold over the legs sharing one `event_id`, and each transition is an INSERT — which is what append-only wants.

The four states need no new vocabulary at all, because MODES (types.ts:31) already encodes exactly the distinctions required:

- **Returned + Refund paid** — a `refund_paid` leg with mode `cash` or `upi`.
- **Returned + Credit issued** — a `refund_paid` leg with mode `udhar`. Party mandatory (credit owed to nobody can never be redeemed — the same argument input.ts:109-112 already makes for udhar sales).
- **Returned + Replacement** — an `exchange_out` leg exists; settled in goods, mode `other`, with any price difference carried by `exchange_receipt` or `refund_paid`.
- **Returned + Pending settlement** — **no settlement leg exists at all.** The state is the absence of a row.

That last one is the point. "Pending" needs no representation, which is why it cannot drift out of sync with reality.

Legal transitions, all append-only and all forward: Pending → any of the three settled states (append a settlement leg). No transition out of a settled state — you cannot un-refund. A settlement entered wrongly is corrected by appending a further linked leg, never by touching the first, and the over-settlement rule in EX-12 is what keeps that honest. Replacement → Refund paid is legal and common (customer takes the replacement, then brings that back too) but it is a *new event*, not a transition of the old one; the two events are independent and each folds to its own state.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/index.ts:111-121 (derivedDayState, derived not latched, with its reason); index.ts:49-61 (sectionStates, same pattern); packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:31 (the five MODES); 0001_init.postgres.up.sql:149-160 (append-only trigger), :240 (REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:400-402 (sqlite equivalent); packages/formula-engine/src/input.ts:109-112 (party mandatory on udhar, the precedent)

**Recommendation.** Write `settlementState(event: readonly Entry[]): 'pending' | 'refunded' | 'credit_issued' | 'replaced'` in the formula engine as a pure fold, next to `derivedDayState`, and store nothing. Put the four-state table in its doc comment and a vector per state in the corpus. No status column, no transition table, no new enum — and say so in the ADR, because a status column is what the next developer will reach for.

### `EX-6` ADR-015 commits each line to SQLite the moment it is entered; a half-entered three-leg exchange is then a business fact that never happened, contradicting the review's §3

**HIGH** · bug

ADR-015 (docs/spec/TECH-DECISIONS.md:622-668) decides what survives process death, and its premise is that "every committed line is an append to local SQLite at the moment it is committed. Those are not 'in memory pending a save' — they are durable before the phone call starts." Committed lines are restored "silently and completely"; only the half-typed numeric field is discarded, visibly.

That is right for the eight existing sections, where one line is one business event — a committed expense really did happen, and persisting it early loses nothing. It is wrong for an exchange. If `sales_return` ₹900 is committed as leg 1 and Android kills the app before `exchange_out` ₹1,200 is entered, the ledger permanently holds a lone ₹900 return: today's Net Sales is down ₹900 for an exchange that never occurred, and the shopkeeper's next reconciliation shows a sales figure he cannot account for.

This is a direct conflict with review §3 — "If the user abandons the form: no transaction exists" — and it is scenario 16 (device/app failure during transaction) landing on the one workflow that cannot absorb it. It is also scenario 14's worst case, because exchange-with-additional-payment is a three-leg form and the money leg is last.

The distinction ADR-015 never had to draw is between a *line* and an *event*.

**Evidence.** docs/spec/TECH-DECISIONS.md:622-668 (ADR-015, "durable before the phone call starts", committed lines restored silently), and :666-668 ("Form state is written to SQLite — not AsyncStorage")

**Recommendation.** Move the commit boundary from the line to the event, for event sections only. An exchange's legs are held in the form-state table that ADR-015 already requires (TECH-DECISIONS.md:666-668 — "Form state is written to SQLite") and are appended to `entries` only on the final confirm, under one local transaction and one `event_id`. Process death restores the *form*, not the ledger, and ADR-015's existing rule applies unchanged inside it: the chosen sections, parties and the two prices come back, the half-typed amount comes back empty with the banner. No new durability tier, no new mechanism — the lazy fix is to use the one already built.

Amend ADR-015 with a short section saying the "committed line" rule holds for the eight closing sections and that an event's legs commit together, and say why. One check: enter leg 1 of an exchange, kill the store, reopen, and assert `entries` is empty while the form shows leg 1.

### `EX-7` Adding the four sections to SECTIONS would push the progress ring to 14 and re-lock the cash check every day for shops that never exchange

**HIGH** · risk

Two constants are coupled to SECTIONS having exactly eight members, both in index.ts:

- `dayProgress` (index.ts:78-94) hardcodes `const TOTAL = 10; // opening cash + 8 entry sections + cash check` and then loops `for (const section of SECTIONS)`. Add four and the ring reports done-out-of-10 against twelve possible increments — a ring that can read 12 of 10.
- `cashCheckUnlocked` (index.ts:103-106) is `SECTIONS.every((s) => states[s] !== 'untouched')`. Add four and a shop that has never done an exchange in its life must tap "Nothing to enter" on four extra sections, every single day, before the cash check will open. That is a direct regression against review §9 (2-4 mandatory steps) and §10 (low cognitive load), and it is the kind of friction a shopkeeper routes around.

SECTIONS is also order-sensitive by spec: spec.ts:43 says "The eight entry sections, in the paper sheet's order. Do NOT reorder (S4 p.3)", and the list is numbered 01-08 at spec.ts:44-53.

An exchange is not a closing section. It is an event that happens during the day and lands *in* the ledger; it does not need to be ticked off before the till can be counted.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/index.ts:79 (TOTAL = 10, hardcoded), :83 (loop over SECTIONS), :103-106 (cashCheckUnlocked = SECTIONS.every); packages/schema/src/spec.ts:43-53 ("Do NOT reorder (S4 p.3)", numbered 01-08); packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:19-29 (the duplicate list the engine uses)

**Recommendation.** Split the vocabulary. Keep `SECTIONS` as the eight **closing sections** that gate the ring and the cash check — untouched, unreordered, still 01-08. Add `EVENT_SECTIONS = ['sales_return','exchange_out','exchange_receipt','refund_paid']`, valid on `entries` and consumed by the formulas, gating nothing. `TOTAL = 10` stays 10; `cashCheckUnlocked` stays over eight; nothing in the ring or the cash check moves.

One test must be widened, not weakened: `packages/schema/test/append-only.spec.ts:312` asserts `Object.keys(SECTION_MODES).sort()` equals `[...SECTIONS].sort()`. Widen the right-hand side to `[...SECTIONS, ...EVENT_SECTIONS].sort()` so every section still has a declared mode set. Do not relax it to a subset or a superset check — that would be exactly the "loosening a matcher" CLAUDE.md forbids, and this assertion is the only thing keeping the mode table and the section list in lockstep. Run `npm run prove:verifiers` afterwards, per CLAUDE.md step 4.

The same split must be applied to the duplicate list at types.ts:19-29, and the lockstep test between the two lists (types.ts:8-10) extended to cover it.

### `EX-8` §4 puts Exchange Receivables, Exchange Payables and Refund Payables inside the CASH equation — the same class of defect as the known 'UPI Sale in Cash Received' one

**HIGH** · decision-needed

Review §4 (pdftotext.txt:108-111) lists, inside the expected-cash formula: `+ Cash Received (Cash Sale/UPI Sale, Credit Collection, Exchange Receivables)` and `- Cash Paid (Expenses, Supplier/Party Payments, Exchange Paybles, Refund Paybles)`.

A receivable is by definition money that has *not* been received, and a payable is money that has *not* been paid. Built literally, expected cash would include the exchange difference a customer promised to bring tomorrow and subtract a refund the shopkeeper has not handed over — so the till could not reconcile on any day with an open exchange or an unsettled return, and `dailyCashHealth` would set `reasonRequired: true` (operational.ts:109-113) for a difference the app created. That is structurally identical to the UPI-Sale defect CLAUDE.md:141-146 already records, and D2 (CLAUDE.md:56-58) is the rule that catches both: only `cash` mode touches the cash equation.

The terms are real and needed — they are just not cash lines. Each splits into a settled part (cash, belongs in P3) and an unsettled part (a balance, belongs beside P7/P8).

**Evidence.** pdftotext.txt:103-116 (§4 expected-cash formula, with Exchange Receivables in Cash Received and Exchange/Refund Payables in Cash Paid); CLAUDE.md:139-146 (the parallel known defect); CLAUDE.md:56-58 (D2); packages/formula-engine/src/operational.ts:109-113 (reasonRequired on any non-zero difference)

**Recommendation.** Flag this for the document's author alongside the UPI-Sale defect — it is the same mistake in three more places and should be confirmed, not silently worked around. Implement the split:

**Cash lines (new working lines on P1/P2/P3, cash mode only):**
- P1 Cash Received `+ exchange_receipt where mode='cash'`
- P2 Cash Paid `+ refund_paid where mode='cash'`
- P3 Expected Cash `+ exchange_receipt(cash) - refund_paid(cash)` — taking its working from 7 lines to 9. That is a feature, not a cost: primary.ts:117-119 says the cash-check screen shows those lines one at a time against the paper sheet, so "refund paid in cash 900" appears as its own line and the owner can see which line is wrong rather than only that something is.

**Balances (new derived figures beside P7/P8, never in P3), folded per event:**
- Exchange Receivable Outstanding = Σ max(0, exchange_out − sales_return − Σexchange_receipt(all modes))
- Exchange Payable Outstanding = Σ max(0, sales_return − exchange_out − Σrefund_paid(all modes))
- Refund Payable Outstanding = Σ (sales_return − Σrefund_paid(all modes)) over events with no exchange_out leg

All three derived on read, no stored totals, consistent with CLAUDE.md:61-64. P6 Total Expenses and estimated profit take no new term at all — a refund is not an expense (EX-2).

### `EX-9` 'Returned + Pending settlement' belongs in P4 immediately, in a payable balance, and nowhere in the cash equation — and a day must be allowed to close with one open

**HIGH** · requirement

Where it appears, and where it must not:

- **P4 Net Sales: immediately and in full.** The goods came back, so the sale is reversed the moment the return is recorded, regardless of whether money moved. Revenue follows the goods; settlement follows the money. That single split *is* the Return-vs-Refund distinction §7 asks for: `sales_return` hits P4 and never P3; `refund_paid` hits P3 and never P4.
- **Cash equation (P1/P2/P3): nowhere.** Nothing has moved. Including it would guarantee a non-zero difference at closing on a day when the drawer is exactly right — the defect in EX-8, self-inflicted.
- **Payables: as Refund Payable Outstanding**, a liability listed beside P8 Supplier Outstanding, aged.

**Can a day close with one open? Yes, and it must.** Four reasons, three of them structural:

1. Mechanically it already can and should. `dailyCashHealth` (operational.ts:95-115) compares counted against expected; a pending refund has moved no cash, so the difference is genuinely 0 and the day is genuinely reconciled. Blocking the close would put a hard gate on a zero difference.
2. §4's close condition is "both differences 0 AND required transactions completed". A pending settlement is not an incomplete transaction — it is a complete business event whose settlement is a *future* event. The customer walked out with no money, and that is the truth of the day the close is supposed to record.
3. D7 would make it unrecoverable. If a pending settlement blocked the close, the day could not close; `nextOpeningCash` (index.ts:175-177) returns null for any day not in state `closed`, so tomorrow's opening cash would be null and the carry-forward chain the architecture calls "where the audit chain is forged" (index.ts:166-171) would break at the first unsettled return.
4. Human behaviour. A shopkeeper takes a shirt back at 8pm and says "come tomorrow, I'll give the money." An app that refuses to close the day until he pays gets closed anyway — by entering a refund that did not happen. A gate users route around is worse than no gate, because the fake row is now indistinguishable from a real one.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/operational.ts:95-115 (dailyCashHealth; difference is 0 when no cash moved); index.ts:175-177 (nextOpeningCash returns null unless state==='closed'); index.ts:164-174 (the carry-forward rule and why it is the audit chain); pdftotext.txt:126-138 (§4 close condition); pdftotext.txt:221-227 (§7 four states)

**Recommendation.** Allow the close, unconditionally, and carry the liability forward as a derived balance. Surface it in the COMPLETE/KNOW zone of the home page (§10) as "unsettled returns: ₹X across N customers", and age it by reusing the vocabulary that already exists rather than inventing a third: `AgedParty` (types.ts:226-237) distinguishes `'overdue'` where a due date exists from `'days_since'` where it does not, "two different words, deliberately" per D4. A pending refund has no promised date, so it is `'days_since'`. Let the Behaviour Engine raise it at §13 Level 4 when it ages ("a return from 9 business days ago is still unsettled") — an observation to review, never an instruction, per CLAUDE.md:76-81.

One vector: a day with a pending return closes with difference 0 and a non-zero Refund Payable Outstanding. One more: the same day's P4 is reduced by the return.

### `EX-10` The first minus term in P4 lets Net Sales go negative, and three downstream consumers silently produce nonsense when it does

**HIGH** · bug

`assertPaise` (money.ts:39-47) checks only integer and safe-integer — negatives pass. `subtract` says so explicitly: "May be negative - a difference legitimately is" (money.ts:56). `figure()` (primary.ts:41-48) has no floor. So once `sales_return` enters P4 with sign '-', a day whose returns exceed its sales produces a negative Net Sales with no error — which is *correct* arithmetic and the right answer, and it is the first time any P value could be negative. Three consumers are not ready for it:

1. `expensePressure` (operational.ts:129-131) is `ratio(totalExpenses, netSales)`. `ratio` (money.ts:102-110) guards only `denominator === 0` → `basisPoints: null`; a negative denominator yields negative basis points. Expense pressure renders as −340%, which means nothing.
2. `businessMovement` (operational.ts:238-253) returns `ratio(today, baseline)`. A negative numerator gives negative basis points. `direction` is still correct ('down'), but the ratio shown beside it is not renderable.
3. `trailingExpensePressure` (operational.ts:134-136) sums the window's sales into the denominator. A negative day pulls that sum toward zero, and at exactly zero `ratio` returns `basisPoints: null` — so the output reads "no baseline" for a window that plainly had activity.

None of these throws. They all return a well-typed `Ratio` with a meaningless number in it, which is the worst failure mode: it reaches the screen.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/money.ts:39-47 (assertPaise permits negatives), :56 (subtract may be negative), :102-110 (ratio guards only denominator===0); packages/formula-engine/src/primary.ts:41-48 (figure has no floor); packages/formula-engine/src/operational.ts:129-131 (OP2), :134-136 (trailing OP2), :238-253 (OP5)

**Recommendation.** Decide the semantics in the ADR before adding the minus term, then encode it once. Conservative reading: a ratio against a non-positive denominator is not a smaller number, it is an *unanswerable question*. Extend `ratio`'s existing guard from `denominator === 0` to `denominator <= 0` → `basisPoints: null`, so all three consumers inherit the fix at one site and the existing GateCard copy (types.ts:144-160, "never a spinner, never a zero") already knows how to say "we cannot tell yet". That is one line and it is the root-cause fix; guarding at each of the three call sites is three times the diff and leaves the fourth caller broken.

Three vectors, all currently impossible to write: a day with negative Net Sales; the same day inside an OP2 window; a trailing window summing to exactly zero sales. Each must fail if the guard is removed.

### `EX-11` A return against a closed day is a new event today, not a correction — and with no item data and no link to the original sale, `sales_return` is an unconstrained negative-revenue lever that the app must never claim to have verified

**HIGH** · security

**The D7 question answers itself from the existing design.** spec.ts:412-413 already states the rule: "A CORRECTION is still allowed, because its own day_id is today (open); only `corrects_day_id` points at the closed day. That is the whole of D7." A return against a sale made on a closed day is not even a correction — nothing about that day was wrong. A sale genuinely happened then; the goods genuinely came back today. So the return's `day_id` is **today**, it carries no `corrects_day_id` at all, the closed day stays byte-identical, and today's P4 falls. That is also the economically right answer: revenue is reversed in the period the goods returned. No new mechanism, and the triggers at 0001_init.postgres.up.sql:175-189 and spec.ts:414-419 need no change — the entry never tries to land in the closed day. (Its consequence is EX-10: today's Net Sales can go negative.)

**The part that is not solved is verification, and it cannot be.** There is no row-level link to the original sale (EX-3), and there is no item data at all — BI5 Inventory Pressure is reserved and permanently null per D5 (types.ts:286-291). So SWAFTO has no way to establish that a returned item was ever sold by this shop, or at what price, or whether it has already been returned once.

Review §7 is already right about this: it says "Authenticity Verification of Claimed Product **by User**" (pdftotext.txt:209). That must stay exactly that way, for precisely the reason §2 gives about UPI: the app records that the shopkeeper confirmed, and never claims it confirmed. "Return verified by SWAFTO" would be the §2 lie in a new costume.

**The abuse surface that follows.** `sales_return` is a direct, unconstrained reduction of Net Sales with no counterparty and no original row to reconcile against. An employee can take cash from the drawer and cover it with a return that reconciles the till exactly. An owner can suppress recorded turnover by any amount. Neither requires defeating a control, because there is no control — and both make the Business Health and Cash Confidence scores (§14) report a healthy shop.

**Evidence.** packages/schema/src/spec.ts:412-413 (the D7 correction rule, verbatim); 0001_init.postgres.up.sql:175-189 and packages/schema/src/spec.ts:414-419 (entries_no_insert_into_closed_day); packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:286-291 (BI5 reserved, always null, D5 — no item data in V1); pdftotext.txt:209 ("by User"); pdftotext.txt:37-53 (§2, never claim SWAFTO verified)

**Recommendation.** Three controls, none of which needs item data and none of which adds a mandatory step to the common case:

1. **Record the confirmation, never the verification.** A mandatory confirm step on `sales_return` whose recorded fact is "the shopkeeper confirmed this item and price" — stored, timestamped, and never phrased as a system check. Mirror the §2 wording rule in the i18n keys: "confirmed by you", never "verified".
2. **Identification where, and only where, the event creates a liability** — which is §9's own rule (pdftotext.txt:241-245: "necessary only when the business event requires identification") applied consistently. Party optional on a return settled on the spot in cash, UPI or goods: that keeps the 2-4 step promise for the common case. Party **mandatory** by CHECK constraint, not UI rule, on (a) `sales_return` with no settlement leg — a pending liability owed to a specific person — and (b) `refund_paid` mode `udhar` — a credit note nobody can redeem is not a credit note. This is the same argument input.ts:109-112 already makes for udhar sales.
3. **Measure the rate and surface it as an observation.** Return value as a share of sales, in §12-D Business Consistency, raised at §13 Level 3/4: "returns were 18% of sales this week, against 3% over the prior four weeks — review recent returns." Never an accusation, never blocking, per CLAUDE.md:76-81 and §13 Level 5. This is the only control that catches the owner-as-actor case, and it is the honest one: it reports a pattern and leaves the decision with the owner.

State plainly in the ADR that SWAFTO cannot detect a fabricated return in V1 and that control 3 is detection-by-pattern only. Do not let the scores imply otherwise.

### `EX-12` Partial refund and exchange-with-additional-payment need no new mechanism, but both depend on one rule the build has no equivalent of: an event may never be settled for more than it is worth

**MEDIUM** · test-case

Both proposed scenarios fall out of the EX-2 model for free:

**Scenario 13, partial refund.** `sales_return` ₹900, `refund_paid` ₹400 cash, one event. P4 −900. Expected cash −400. Fold → settled ₹400 of ₹900, so Refund Payable Outstanding ₹500. The derived state is "partially settled", which is not a fifth state — it is the same `pending` fold with a non-zero settled amount, and §7's four states remain four.

**Scenario 14, exchange with additional payment.** The ₹900/₹1,200/₹300 case in EX-2. The mirror case — replacement cheaper, say ₹1,200 returned for a ₹900 replacement — needs no fifth section: `refund_paid` ₹300 covers "the shop pays the customer", for returns and exchanges alike. That is why the model needs four sections rather than five.

What both depend on is a constraint with no precedent in the build: **Σ settlement legs ≤ Σ returned value, per event.** Without it, a double-tapped refund button pays ₹400 twice against a ₹900 return and the drawer is ₹800 short with the ledger claiming it is right. There is nothing like this today — every existing CHECK (0001_init.postgres.up.sql:83-103) constrains a single row against constants, never a row against the sum of its siblings, and `appendEntry` (postgres.ts:291-322) is a bare insert. It cannot be a CHECK constraint; it has to be a rule evaluated against the event's existing legs at append time.

It is also the structural fix for scenario 10 (duplicate transaction) on this workflow, which is otherwise the easiest way in the whole app to lose money.

**Evidence.** pdftotext.txt:432-438 (scenarios 12-16 proposed); pdftotext.txt:221-227 (§7 four states); 0001_init.postgres.up.sql:83-103 (every entries CHECK is single-row); apps/api/src/db/postgres.ts:291-322 (appendEntry, bare INSERT with no sibling read)

**Recommendation.** Put the rule in the formula engine as a pure predicate — `canSettle(event: readonly Entry[], proposed: Entry): boolean` — so it is testable by vector and runs identically on phone and server (ADR-012), and call it from both the local commit path and `appendEntry`. Over-settlement is a refusal with a named field, in the style of `EntryInputError` (input.ts:32-39), so the message lands beside the input: "this return has already been settled in full."

Five vectors, which are also the five scenarios: partial refund; exchange with additional payment; exchange with a refund due; duplicate refund refused; a return settled in two instalments across two days, the second after the first day closed (EX-11). Each must fail if `canSettle` is removed.

Skipped: a redemption link so a later sale can consume a credit note. Add when a shopkeeper actually needs "use ₹300 credit" — until then the credit note is a party balance he reads and applies himself.

### `EX-13` A credit note must not be netted into P7 Credit Outstanding, or P7's working line starts meaning two different things

**MEDIUM** · requirement

`creditOutstanding` (primary.ts:214-225) is three working lines: prior outstanding + udhar sales − udhar collections in all modes. Its comment (primary.ts:211-212) is precise about why collections use the section total while P1 uses the cash slice.

A credit note issued on a return (`refund_paid` mode `udhar`) is the shop owing the *customer*. That is the opposite direction from P7, which measures what customers owe the shop. Netting it in would be wrong in two ways: P7 could go negative and read as "customers owe −₹400", and the working line "prior + sales − collections" would no longer explain the number on screen, which is the one thing primary.ts:117-119 and the whole `Working` type (types.ts:112-132) exist to guarantee. It is also only *sometimes* a reduction — if the customer owed nothing, a ₹400 credit note creates a liability rather than reducing an asset, and a single netted figure cannot tell those apart.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/src/primary.ts:208-225 (P7 and its three working lines); primary.ts:117-119 (the working is what the cash-check screen renders, line by line); packages/formula-engine/src/types.ts:112-132 (Working exists so the owner can see which line is wrong)

**Recommendation.** Give it its own derived figure — Customer Credit Notes Outstanding = Σ `refund_paid` mode `udhar`, grouped by party — listed as a liability beside P8, not netted into P7. P7's three lines stay exactly as they are and its working keeps explaining its number. Conservative, separable, and reversible if a later decision says to net them.

One vector: a customer with ₹0 udhar receives a ₹400 credit note; assert P7 is unchanged at 0 and the new figure is 400.

### `EX-14` Unlike the retailer-defined business day, this change does not invalidate the 77-vector corpus — so land it first

**LOW** · decision-needed

Measured: `packages/formula-engine/vectors.json` holds 77 vectors. CLAUDE.md:120-127 records that decision 1 (retailer-defined business day) is a breaking change to `dates.ts`, D7 and the 77-vector corpus, and that every existing `day_id` needs a migration.

Exchange and Return/Refund are not in that class. Every existing vector has no `sales_return`, `exchange_out`, `exchange_receipt` or `refund_paid` entry, so each new term sums over an empty set to 0, and `subtract(x, 0) === x` and `add(x, 0) === x` under money.ts:50-60. Every existing vector's expected output is therefore byte-identical, and `event_id` is NULL on every existing row. The corpus is extended, not migrated.

That makes the sequencing decision easy and worth stating explicitly, because the two V1 decisions look equally disruptive from the outside and are not.

**Evidence.** packages/formula-engine/vectors.json — 77 vectors (counted); CLAUDE.md:120-127 (decision 1 breaks the corpus and needs a day_id migration); packages/formula-engine/src/money.ts:50-60 (add/subtract identities); packages/formula-engine/src/primary.ts:41-48 (figure folds over lines, zero-safe)

**Recommendation.** Land Exchange and Return/Refund before the retailer-defined business day, not after. This work needs no corpus migration and can be verified entirely by new vectors against a green baseline; decision 1 rewrites `day_id` across all 77 and will churn every expected output it touches. Doing the day change first means debugging return arithmetic against a corpus that is itself in motion. Record the ordering in the ADR so it is a decision rather than an accident, and add one vector asserting that an exchange event's legs all share one `event_id` and that the eight existing sections still carry NULL.
