# SWAFTO Retail Desk redesign, M1: three visual directions

Author: product-designer, 2026-11-18. Structure is fixed by redesign v1 and the research (sale-first Today with pinned tiles; one hero "In the box now"; balances first in the Ledger; quiet skip with undo in day close; a sync chip that is never red; a strong Add above the keypad). Only the visual language varies. Judgements are expert review; no shopkeeper has seen these (assumption until M4). Odia copy is the unreviewed draft from `strings.ts` (H13).

Files (open in a browser; Google Fonts is the only external request): `A-khata.html`, `B-board.html`, `C-mitra.html`. Each shows Today, Sale entry with keypad, Day close and Ledger at 360 x 800, an en/od toggle, a Comfort/Focus toggle, state specimens, and the full spec. A also shows the text-size picker. They were generated by one script, so the three share logic and copy; only palette, CSS, icons and a few markup choices differ.

## Recommendation

**Build A (Khata) as the base, and borrow four pictograms from C for the four pinned tiles only, subject to the CTO accepting a small icon font. If the CTO declines the icon font, build A alone.**

Reasons:
1. It is the only direction that passes the audit yardstick with token-value changes alone (Phase A: values change, names do not), so it is the cheapest to get right on a ₹7,000 phone and inside `check:layout`, `check:font` and `check:design`.
2. The user's core fear is getting the money wrong. A ledger grammar (ruled lines, double-underlined totals, signs and words on every figure) is the closest visual match to the paper khata he already trusts, and it makes the hero "In the box now" read as a total, not a dashboard.
3. B is the best in glare but is the most likely to feel cheap or loud for a product holding someone's cash, and it spends the most vertical space, which is the binding constraint on entry screens. C is the best for scanning but carries the largest cost (a new font asset, a check change, five new state colours) before anyone has shown that icons speed the 10-second sale. Taking only the four tile pictograms from C captures most of its low-literacy benefit for a fraction of the cost (about 5 glyphs).
4. It is a decision that M4 can still overturn: A's tokens (palette, radius, border) are values, so changing direction later is cheap; C's icon font is not.

Open questions for Saket: (a) accept an icon font of about 5 to 20 glyphs (approach A in the audit), or stay View-drawn only; (b) is a darker paper ground (#F7F1E3) acceptable, to be tested in daylight on the real phone; (c) the Odia labels for the text-size picker (Normal / Large / Extra large) do not exist in `strings.ts` and need a native reviewer (H13).

## Cross-cutting notes
- Layout facts the mockups hold to: 56 dp targets (keypad keys are 56 high; back, chip, segments, rows 56 or more), 17 sp minimum text, 1.8 leading on text, no letter-spacing, no all-caps, no gradients, no blur, no shadows. Latin-digit money uses leading 1.4 to fit the budget; the app token is 1.8 and that gap is flagged above.
- Informational rows on the Sale screen (the last two sales, 48 high) are not tappable, so they are not held to 56.
- Status is never colour alone: sync chip = icon + words, day-close marks = tick / dash / lock / number + words, selected mode = tick + fill + bold, money = printed sign + words.
- Red is not used for money in any direction. Sync waiting is amber with a clock icon and the words "safe on phone".
- Mockup caveat: browser render with a variable Anek Odia from Google Fonts. Not verified on the vivo I2219. Odia at 1.5x and 2.0x, conjunct clipping and tabular digits on Android are M2/M3 checks. Not visually reviewed by me after generation; only the script and contrast numbers were machine-checked.

Ground: every concept uses a warm, non-white ground. #F7F1E3 (A), #FFFBEA (B), #FFF6EA (C); pure white is only a card. Reason (assumption, to be confirmed by the M4 daylight test on the real phone): an all-white screen is the brightest possible surface in a sunlit shop, and the audit records that the current warm paper was chosen to cut that glare. A is the darkest ground (relative luminance 0.882, against 0.952 for today's #FFF9EF) and is the largest daylight change proposed; B and C stay within 0.962 to 0.931.

**Type.** Anek Odia only (the bundled family; static Regular / Medium / Bold are the only weights used, so no new font, no check:font change, +0 bytes). Latin, digits and the rupee sign render in the same face. Money is Medium with tabular figures (`tnum`); body 17, heading 21, figure 24-34, hero 36. Leading 1.8 on all text. No letter-spacing, no all-caps. The mockup loads the Google Fonts (variable) Anek Odia for preview only; the app uses its own static files.

**Icons.** View-drawn marks only (approach B in the audit): tick, dash, lock, clock, chevron, back, backspace, plus (8 marks, 0 bytes, 0 native modules). Tiles carry words, not pictograms. Status marks never stand alone: each sits beside its word.

**Shape and depth.** Square corners (radius 4 control, 2 card). Depth by fill step and hairline: ground, card, raised. No elevation anywhere in the mockup (the system's 2 and 8 would still be available). Edges: 1.5 px in the muted-ink edge colour for controls, 1 px rule colour for separators. The accounting double rule under the hero figure, the header and the tab bar is the signature; in React Native it is two Views (Android has no double border style).

**Rationale for this user.** This direction treats the money figures as the product. A shopkeeper who has kept a paper khata for thirty years already trusts a ruled page, a total with a double underline and a running list that reads top to bottom, so the screen borrows that grammar instead of asking him to learn a new one. The warm paper ground cuts the glare of a bright shop front, the ink is near black and heavy, and nothing relies on a pale tint or a thin stroke, which a sun-washed screen erases first. One hand: the biggest control is the indigo Sale bar at the top and the full-width Add bar sits directly above the keypad on the thumb side. Low literacy: icons are deliberately absent from the tiles, which is the weakness of this direction and the reason the recommendation borrows C's pictograms for four tiles. Trust: green and brick are always paired with the printed sign and the words "cash in" / "cash out", never colour alone.

**Risks.**
- Low-literacy scanning is weakest here: four tiles are words only. Mitigation: adopt four pictograms from C (needs an icon font, see C).
- Brick (#8A3A10) for money out is close to red; the sign and the word carry the meaning and red proper is not used for money anywhere. Needs a check with a red-green colour-blind reader in M4.
- The double rule is cheap in Views but will not survive a quick port as a border style; it must be drawn as two Views.
- Hero 36 vs today's hero token 32: one step larger, and Latin-digit money at 1.4 leading (the app token is 1.8): needs a figure-leading token and a check:layout run (keypad fold at 1.5x).
- The calm look is the least distinctive; the risk is that it reads as "the old app, tidied".

**Audit yardstick.** Yes, with value-only changes (Phase A in the audit): hero 36 (token is 32) and a Latin-digit figure leading of about 1.4 (token is 1.8) need a token step and a check:layout run; the ground moves from #FFF9EF to #F7F1E3 (about 5% darker, justified below). No font, icon, native-module or check change. Two View-drawn double rules.

**Type.** Anek Odia only, Bold for almost everything (static Bold is already bundled, no new file). Body 17-19, tiles 19, hero 44, figures 22-40, leading 1.8. Tabular figures on. No letter-spacing, no all-caps (a signboard look in Odia comes from weight and size, not case). Money is Bold. Hero 44 and figure 40 exceed today's tokens (32 / 24): flagged.

**Icons.** Heavy geometric marks (3.6 px stroke at 24): tick, dash, plus, chevron, back, backspace, up and down triangles for cash in and out, and small pictograms on tiles. Audit approach B (View-drawn) is enough for tick, dash, plus, chevron, back, backspace, triangles; lock, clock and the four tile pictograms need an icon font (approach A, about 10 glyphs, bytes unmeasured). Words are always present.

**Shape and depth.** No radius. 3 px black edge on every control, with a 6 px bottom edge as the "raised sign" (a border, not a shadow, so no GPU cost; a tap swaps it to 3 px). Hard yellow (#FFD21F) fill for the action and header; a black hero block with a yellow figure. No elevation, no gradient, no blur. Needs a second border weight token (3) beside the single 1 px hairline.

**Rationale for this user.** This direction is built for the worst light and the worst eyes. Shop signboards in Odisha are black on yellow or white because they are read from across a road in direct sun, and the same contrast holds on a cheap, dim phone screen outdoors. Everything is large, heavy and boxed, so a tap target cannot be missed one-handed, and "In the box now" is a yellow figure on a black block that cannot be mistaken for anything else. Low literacy is served by size and position rather than by icons: the same big boxes always sit in the same places. Trust in money: figures are the largest thing on every screen, signs are printed, and out-amounts are never red. The cost is tone: it shouts, and a numerate, middle-aged owner may find it childish or cheap for something that holds his money.

**Risks.**
- Tone: may read as cheap or loud for a product that holds a shop's cash. M4 should ask.
- Vertical budget: tall tiles (well above the touch floor), a large hero figure and thick 3-6 px edges eat the keypad fold at 1.5x text, which check:layout already polices.
- Yellow-on-cream cannot define a boundary (1.2:1), so every yellow fill is edged in black; if the edge is dropped in a port, the 3:1 control-edge rule fails.
- The ground is cream (#FFFBEA), not white, deliberately: pure white is the glare the audit warns about.
- A second border token (3 px) and bigger type steps are token additions, not value-only changes.

**Audit yardstick.** With changes: hero 44 and figure 40 exceed the type tokens; a 3 px border weight token is new; ground moves to cream #FFFBEA; icon font needed for lock, clock and tile pictograms (about 10 glyphs, bytes unmeasured). Fonts: Anek Bold only, no check change. Highest risk to the keypad-above-fold check at 1.5x.

**Type.** Anek Odia only. An earlier draft used Baloo Bhaina 2 for friendliness; it is dropped because the audit makes a second Odia family a check:font change and about +490 KB, and money must stay in the Odia face. Friendliness comes from shape, tint and pictograms instead. Bold for money and labels, Medium for body, leading 1.8, tabular figures, no tracking, no all-caps.

**Icons.** Icon font (audit approach A): about 20 glyphs (coin, notebook, tray, receipt, dots, box, bars, note, phone, user, tick, dash, lock, clock, check, chevron, back, backspace, plus, up/down). Cost: one new static .ttf in assets/fonts (size not measured; ASSUMPTION 30-60 KB), a check:font and fonts-packaged generalisation, a licence check, and a test double. Every pictogram sits beside its word and takes its accessible name from the existing string. Colour is a third cue, never the only one.

**Shape and depth.** Soft: radius 20 on cards and tiles, 24 on the header, hero and tab bar, pill (28-32) on buttons, segments and chips; pictogram discs below the touch floor (decorative, inside a full-size tile); circular avatar initials in the Ledger. Depth by tint only (5 tint pairs per mode), no elevation, no shadow, no gradient. 2 px edges at 3:1 on controls.

**Rationale for this user.** This direction is for the owner who reads slowly, or whose helper or daughter reads for him. A coin, a notebook, a hand-and-tray and a receipt let him find Sale, Udhar sale, Collect and Expense by shape before he reads the word, and each also has its own tint so the four jobs stay apart even at arm's length. Soft rounded shapes and warm cream make the app feel like a helper rather than an accounts system, which matters for adoption by someone who is slightly afraid of getting money wrong. The Sale tile is the only solid teal block, so the one-hand, forty-times-a-day action is still first. Trust is kept by the figures, which are plain, large and signed; the friendliness is in the frame, not in the numbers.

**Risks.**
- Icon font is the one real cost: a new asset, a check change (prove:verifiers), a licence review, and 20 glyphs to keep consistent. If the CTO declines, only the View-drawable marks survive and this direction loses its point.
- Five tint pairs times two modes are new state colours that must be added to tokens.spec with the same names in both modes.
- Five hues can become a rainbow; decorative colour must not be read as meaning (no tile hue means "good" or "bad").
- Odia initials in avatar discs need a check on conjunct-initial names (a first code point is not always a whole letter).
- Large radii and many rounded Views cost slightly more to draw than rectangles on a low-end GPU, but there are no shadows or blur.

**Audit yardstick.** With changes: needs an icon font (about 20 glyphs, bytes unmeasured, ASSUMPTION 30-60 KB; check:font and fonts-packaged generalised; licence review; test double); five new tint pairs per mode in tokens; radius 20/28 tokens (xl 24 exists); hero 36 and a figure-leading token as in A. Anek only, so no second Odia family.
