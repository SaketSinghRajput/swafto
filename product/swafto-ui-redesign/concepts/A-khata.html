Ratios are computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula by the generator script that wrote this file; any pair under its threshold stops generation. Large-text threshold 3:1 is not relied on anywhere: every text pair is held to 4.5:1.

Ground: every concept uses a warm, non-white ground. #F7F1E3 (A), #FFFBEA (B), #FFF6EA (C); pure white is only a card. Reason (assumption, to be confirmed by the M4 daylight test on the real phone): an all-white screen is the brightest possible surface in a sunlit shop, and the audit records that the current warm paper was chosen to cut that glare. A is the darkest ground (relative luminance 0.882, against 0.952 for today's #FFF9EF) and is the largest daylight change proposed; B and C stay within 0.962 to 0.931.

Type

Anek Odia only (the bundled family; static Regular / Medium / Bold are the only weights used, so no new font, no check:font change, +0 bytes). Latin, digits and the rupee sign render in the same face. Money is Medium with tabular figures (`tnum`); body 17, heading 21, figure 24-34, hero 36. Leading 1.8 on all text. No letter-spacing, no all-caps. The mockup loads the Google Fonts (variable) Anek Odia for preview only; the app uses its own static files.

Icons

View-drawn marks only (approach B in the audit): tick, dash, lock, clock, chevron, back, backspace, plus (8 marks, 0 bytes, 0 native modules). Tiles carry words, not pictograms. Status marks never stand alone: each sits beside its word.

Shape, edge and depth

Square corners (radius 4 control, 2 card). Depth by fill step and hairline: ground, card, raised. No elevation anywhere in the mockup (the system's 2 and 8 would still be available). Edges: 1.5 px in the muted-ink edge colour for controls, 1 px rule colour for separators. The accounting double rule under the hero figure, the header and the tab bar is the signature; in React Native it is two Views (Android has no double border style).

Why this for this user

This direction treats the money figures as the product. A shopkeeper who has kept a paper khata for thirty years already trusts a ruled page, a total with a double underline and a running list that reads top to bottom, so the screen borrows that grammar instead of asking him to learn a new one. The warm paper ground cuts the glare of a bright shop front, the ink is near black and heavy, and nothing relies on a pale tint or a thin stroke, which a sun-washed screen erases first. One hand: the biggest control is the indigo Sale bar at the top and the full-width Add bar sits directly above the keypad on the thumb side. Low literacy: icons are deliberately absent from the tiles, which is the weakness of this direction and the reason the recommendation borrows C's pictograms for four tiles. Trust: green and brick are always paired with the printed sign and the words "cash in" / "cash out", never colour alone.

Risks

  • Low-literacy scanning is weakest here: four tiles are words only. Mitigation: adopt four pictograms from C (needs an icon font, see C).
  • Brick (#8A3A10) for money out is close to red; the sign and the word carry the meaning and red proper is not used for money anywhere. Needs a check with a red-green colour-blind reader in M4.
  • The double rule is cheap in Views but will not survive a quick port as a border style; it must be drawn as two Views.
  • Hero 36 vs today's hero token 32: +4 dp, and Latin-digit money at 1.4 leading (the app token is 1.8): needs a figure-leading token and a check:layout run (keypad fold at 1.5x).
  • The calm look is the least distinctive; the risk is that it reads as "the old app, tidied".

Audit yardstick

Yes, with value-only changes (Phase A in the audit): hero 36 (token is 32) and a Latin-digit figure leading of about 1.4 (token is 1.8) need a token step and a check:layout run; the ground moves from #FFF9EF to #F7F1E3 (about 5% darker, justified below). No font, icon, native-module or check change. Two View-drawn double rules.
