Ratios are computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula by the generator script that wrote this file; any pair under its threshold stops generation. Large-text threshold 3:1 is not relied on anywhere: every text pair is held to 4.5:1.

Ground: every concept uses a warm, non-white ground. #F7F1E3 (A), #FFFBEA (B), #FFF6EA (C); pure white is only a card. Reason (assumption, to be confirmed by the M4 daylight test on the real phone): an all-white screen is the brightest possible surface in a sunlit shop, and the audit records that the current warm paper was chosen to cut that glare. A is the darkest ground (relative luminance 0.882, against 0.952 for today's #FFF9EF) and is the largest daylight change proposed; B and C stay within 0.962 to 0.931.

Type

Anek Odia only, Bold for almost everything (static Bold is already bundled, no new file). Body 17-19, tiles 19, hero 44, figures 22-40, leading 1.8. Tabular figures on. No letter-spacing, no all-caps (a signboard look in Odia comes from weight and size, not case). Money is Bold. Hero 44 and figure 40 exceed today's tokens (32 / 24): flagged.

Icons

Heavy geometric marks (3.6 px stroke at 24): tick, dash, plus, chevron, back, backspace, up and down triangles for cash in and out, and small pictograms on tiles. Audit approach B (View-drawn) is enough for tick, dash, plus, chevron, back, backspace, triangles; lock, clock and the four tile pictograms need an icon font (approach A, about 10 glyphs, bytes unmeasured). Words are always present.

Shape, edge and depth

No radius. 3 px black edge on every control, with a 6 px bottom edge as the "raised sign" (a border, not a shadow, so no GPU cost; a tap swaps it to 3 px). Hard yellow (#FFD21F) fill for the action and header; a black hero block with a yellow figure. No elevation, no gradient, no blur. Needs a second border weight token (3) beside the single 1 px hairline.

Why this for this user

This direction is built for the worst light and the worst eyes. Shop signboards in Odisha are black on yellow or white because they are read from across a road in direct sun, and the same contrast holds on a cheap, dim phone screen outdoors. Everything is large, heavy and boxed, so a tap target cannot be missed one-handed, and "In the box now" is a yellow figure on a black block that cannot be mistaken for anything else. Low literacy is served by size and position rather than by icons: the same big boxes always sit in the same places. Trust in money: figures are the largest thing on every screen, signs are printed, and out-amounts are never red. The cost is tone: it shouts, and a numerate, middle-aged owner may find it childish or cheap for something that holds his money.

Risks

  • Tone: may read as cheap or loud for a product that holds a shop's cash. M4 should ask.
  • Vertical budget: 76 dp tiles, 44 dp hero and 3-6 px edges eat the keypad fold at 1.5x text, which check:layout already polices.
  • Yellow-on-cream cannot define a boundary (1.2:1), so every yellow fill is edged in black; if the edge is dropped in a port, the 3:1 control-edge rule fails.
  • The ground is cream (#FFFBEA), not white, deliberately: pure white is the glare the audit warns about.
  • A second border token (3 px) and bigger type steps are token additions, not value-only changes.

Audit yardstick

With changes: hero 44 and figure 40 exceed the type tokens; a 3 px border weight token is new; ground moves to cream #FFFBEA; icon font needed for lock, clock and tile pictograms (about 10 glyphs, bytes unmeasured). Fonts: Anek Bold only, no check change. Highest risk to the keypad-above-fold check at 1.5x.

Mockup honesty: rendered in a browser with Google Fonts Anek Odia (variable), not on the Android phone. Layout, Odia rendering at 1.5x/2.0x and tabular figures on device are M2/M3 checks.
